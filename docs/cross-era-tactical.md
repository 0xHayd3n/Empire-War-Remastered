# Cross-era factions in Skirmish and Instant Battle

**Implemented and verified: the first matchup.** On stock Abregado-rae, Revan’s Revenge’s Sith Empire and Fall of the Republic’s Trade Federation damaged opposing mobile units in all eight runs: solo Skirmish and Instant Battle, on land and in space, with each faction played by the human. In Skirmish, choose the two seats under **Teams**; in Instant Battle use **Forces**, then **Edit fleets** for space or the two faction selectors for land. Instant Battle uses the same revision-pinned guest kit as Skirmish. This is a result for these two installed revisions, not a compatibility claim for every roster, armor interaction or saved playset. The manifest and damage-mapping design below is not fully implemented.

**Decision:** one battlefield host, one simulation, independently sourced factions. Import the combatants, not another game's installation. [Decision and alternatives](adr/0001-cross-era-tactical-rosters.md) · [Terms](../GLOSSARY.md) · [Installed playset precedence](installed-compatibility.md).

## Installed roster coverage

The seven primary sources now offer **75 land and 75 space tactical seats** (75 distinct profile/faction entries). An otherwise empty domain can use troops explicitly assigned to that faction on a campaign world, or mobile units deployed by one of its bases. The extractor records the originating campaign or garrison; it never starts the campaign or runs its scripts. Existing direct-affiliation kits take precedence. Fall of the Republic marks `Underworld` unused and does not supply it a native fleet: the exception is labelled **Universal pirate-base auxiliaries** and uses that pack’s own pirate-base wings. The auxiliary is disabled if the reviewed declaration changes. Thrawn’s Revenge is a different case: its `Underworld` space kit now lists four TIE units deployed by shared starbases, labelled **Shared starbase garrison**. The old `Cheat_Space_Unit` identifier resolves for saved forces but is no longer an opening or purchase. Compiled space bodies and projectiles retain the authored `Min_Speed`, `Max_Rate_Of_Turn`, and `Max_Rate_Of_Roll`.

`node tools/check-tactical-pair-setups.mjs --host stock` checks both modes, both domains and same-faction opponents on that host; repeat with each primary pack ID. In this installation all 79,800 setup checks passed (75 × 76 / 2 pairs × 2 domains × 2 modes × 7 hosts). This proves that the selected sources resolve to real opening units, not that every battle finishes or is balanced. In mounted land trials, some armies on the Abregado-rae campaign field stalled or dealt only one-way damage; the same factions exchanged damage on the Dantooine multiplayer field. Prefer a purpose-built skirmish field when testing whether armies can reach one another.

A separate headless **space contact** gate tested 5,625 ordered attacker→defender pairs using the defender’s installed source as host. Every direction produced positive shield or hull loss from a real mobile projectile: 5,601 on the first product pairing, 24 with alternate ships. This used legal attack orders, loaded collision geometry and up to 30 simulated seconds per attempt. Reversing a direction can change the host or ships: it is **not** 2,850 demonstrated two-way fights in one battle. Nor is contact proof of donor balance: in 3,578 passing directions at least one damaged layer had no explicit damage/armor entry in the host table.

On **stock Abregado-rae in space**, a second check put both fleets in the same simulation with ordinary attack orders. All **2,850 unordered pairs**, including 75 mirrors, dealt positive projectile damage both ways in the same encounter. The first ship pairing worked for 2,779; the other 71 used alternate ships or fleets. Two complete runs reproduced the same results. This is a headless combat check on one map and seed, not 2,850 mounted Skirmish launches or Instant Battles, and not a guarantee for every possible fleet.

**Ground coverage is partial.** The early Tatooine checker sometimes stopped at projectile launch, before impact; its combined directional results must not be used as a same-battle pass count. Ground kits now preserve authored turret/muzzle bones, swivel limits and axes for both vehicles and hardpoints. On the stock Dantooine multiplayer map, the updated Chiss OMPAI and native Imperial stormtroopers exchanged hull damage in mounted Instant Battle. Skirmish also passed with the human commanding the Empire (16.33 hull damage dealt by the Empire and 482.39 by the Chiss). With the human commanding the Chiss, the OMPAI damaged opposing infantry but no return loss was recorded. Do not count that opening as two-way combat.

**Dantooine ground panel: 189/189.** The panel fielded all 75 entries as detachments, including stock forces (not the native stock UI path). The former 30 misses now have two independent same-duel passes apiece; the earlier 159 were rerun with the new kits. All 32 different-source pairs in the panel passed. The land extractor now carries authored movement class, flight height, lift and acceleration, so a flying unit is no longer placed and simulated as ground-bound solely because it is a guest. The gun’s original facing and target restrictions are unchanged. Alternative company pairings and engagement bearings mattered; not every starting force or approach exchanged damage. The remaining 2,661 ground pairs were not tested by this panel.

To use a tested guest unit on land in solo Instant Battle, open **Forces**, select each faction, then choose **Your company** and **Opponent company**. **Default companies** keeps the original automatic army. The chosen company is validated against the current seat’s own roster; changing factions resets it. Native forces and multiplayer setup are unchanged. Skirmish uses its normal openings and production.

## What crosses between mods

| Battlefield host owns | A faction source supplies | Never combined |
|---|---|---|
| Map and its objects; environment; simulation and AI scheduler; match objectives; Skirmish bases and economy | Selected units, squad members, movement, weapons, projectiles, hardpoints, targeting/collision, local damage/armor semantics, eligible abilities, art and localized labels | Donor campaign, Lua, global constants, map CRC registry, victory scripts or unqualified names from two overhauls |

A team is an alliance, **not a roster key**. Two enemies can even have the same faction and still be enemies. Same-team players must not auto-target each other; existing authored splash/friendly-fire rules remain separate. In mixed Instant Battle, a teammate no longer forces a different seat to switch factions. Preserve current native-only behavior for existing drafts and saves.

## One match contract

```text
Universal setup:  battlefield + seats + mode-specific choices
                                |
                     resolveMatch(draft, catalog)
                        /                    \
               issues per seat         pinned BattleManifest
                                              |
                           prepareBattle(manifest, localSources)
                                              |
                           battle-local roster + resolved setup
                             /                              \
             Skirmish: bases, purchases, AI       Instant: deploy armies, eliminate
                             \                              /
                            existing land or space simulation
```

These are proposed interfaces. `resolveMatch` does not touch the game database. The menu can list available products from catalog summaries; no model or entire donor database loads on a search or hover. `prepareBattle` verifies selected kits and all required references in a temporary registry, then publishes the complete registry in one operation. If any seat fails, keep the draft, discard the temporary registry, identify the seat and item, and offer **change faction**, **change field**, or **retry**. Never invent a stock stand-in.

The manifest records:

- **Host:** immutable source key, domain (`land` or `space`), exact map file or generated-map recipe/seed, mode, rules version and match seed. `mode` here means Skirmish or Instant; at the engine adapter it becomes the existing `setup.mode` **domain** plus `setup.instant` where appropriate.
- **Seat:** stable seat ID, team ID, controller/difficulty and paint; a playing seat has one `FactionRef = (sourceKey, sourceFactionId)`. Spectators have no faction or army. For example, the Sith seat is `(Revan@revision, Rebel)`, *not* the stock Rebellion; the other is `(Fall@revision, Trade_Federation)`.
- **Armies:** source-qualified product IDs and counts **by seat**, with budget/slot constraints, for Instant. Randomize resolves once to an explicit legal list. Skirmish instead records its starting conditions; the same resolved products feed opening forces, production and AI purchases. Never use team number as the army array index.
- **Integrity:** schema, extractor and interaction-policy versions; digests of required kits and the resolved simulation inputs. Source keys include edition/base fingerprint and **ordered** `(Workshop ID, installed content revision)` layers, not display names, ports, Steam IDs alone, or merely `stock`. A different submod order is a different source. Keep localized labels and cosmetic settings out of the simulation digest.

**Native fast path:** where all selected seats can be represented natively and there is no mixed-roster request, pass the existing setup through unchanged. In a mixed battle a home seat can use native definitions; a visiting seat uses a qualified registry. All lookups for a projectile, its hardpoint, model, or texture retain their originating source. Runtime keys must distinguish kind, source and original ID (and faction-specific wrappers where needed). Durable references must not be concatenated display strings. No donor type enters the list enumerated by host maps, campaigns or normal skirmish discovery.

## A roster is more than a list of ships

Build a versioned, data-only kit from **effective** definitions after source inheritance and submod precedence. Traverse the references required to spawn, move, aim, fire and receive damage: a company/squad → members → weapons/hardpoints → projectile and hit behavior; also armor, shield, collision geometry, targeting masks, firing arcs/bones, timings, and required model/animation dependencies. Use typed, bounded traversal with deduplication and explicit handling of cycles; never evaluate donor scripts. Reuse the scoped `UXA` art path, including nested references. Cache by immutable source, faction, domain and compiler version. Bump the solo-atlas cache schema when the extractor changes: updating the code alone must not reuse a kit missing collision fields.

**Collision contract:** distinguish authored `false`, inherited `true`, and missing. Materialize the effective value using the source object's rules. Keep intentional non-targets and decoys non-targetable. If a combat-critical property or required dependency cannot be represented, exclude that product with a reason. Missing decorative FX may degrade visibly without changing a damage roll. A source with no usable attacking force for the selected domain is unavailable there; a campaign portrait or a space-only faction does not imply a land army.

**Damage contract:** within one source use its authored damage vs. armor/shield table, including explicit zeroes. Across sources, map each source's damage and armor classes through a versioned compatibility table to shared classes; use a reviewed cross-source multiplier for the pair. The host's stock units, stations and destructible map objects participate under the host's source key. Resolve and cache these pairs *before* launching: no network calls or string matching per shot. Unknown custom classes are **unmapped**, not equivalent because their XML names match, and not silently `1×` because the native `db.damageMod()` defaults to it. Gate that interaction or product until mapped and explain why. Legitimate immunities stay zero. Compatibility promises a functional matchup, not identical damage statistics or tournament balance.

## Integration and rollout

1. **Repair the Skirmish reference fight first.** Extend `tools/skirmish-rosters.mjs` and the [atlas kit cache](../tools/universal-atlas.mjs). Ship field parity and direct-hit tests before widening menus. Keep the first kit bounded (today's extractor selects up to eight products); list included and omitted products. Expand coverage deliberately rather than calling eight units a whole tech tree.
2. **Extract the shared preparation path.** Adapt `skirmishsetup.js`, `skirmishdetachments.js` and `gamedata.js` to resolve the new manifest. Both `main.js`/`landgame.js` and `spacebattleworker.js` must prepare **the same** registry before the first entity is constructed; the worker acknowledges the expected digest before unpause. Scope registry, name, art and roster caches to the battle; on exit or failed remount, release them. Keep one simulation clock and one owner for damage.
3. **Enable mixed Instant Battle behind a solo-only flag.** Reuse the Skirmish source picker on **Forces** and in **Edit fleets**. Adapt `instantbattle.js` so eligibility, costs, presets and random armies read the selected seat's product list, not a faction-name-only cache. Keep native fleet budgeting and elimination; don't manufacture a space station to make an Instant roster available. For land, resolve `groundArmies` from each chosen faction instead of looking both up in the host's `groundForces`. Preserve normal seat/team limits. Changing maps transfers only the draft; revalidate domain, budget and revision on the destination and highlight invalid picks rather than dropping them.
4. **Persist, expand, then enable by default.** Store the manifest alongside tactical saves and replay setup. On restore, prepare and verify it **before** resolving entity IDs; retain stable IDs or an explicit old→new mapping. Continue reading native saves unchanged. Adapt legacy `skirmishRosters` using their pinned donor revision and legacy key mapping; if equivalent data cannot be recovered, keep the save and report the mismatch. A mod or rules update must invalidate certification and caches, not silently change a resumed battle. Rollback means disabling new mixed launches while retaining save readers. Stage stock + six primary sources first; then index exact enabled playsets as sources with their own effective kits. Workshop playset windows currently lack the Universal atlas: wire a read-only catalog/asset resolver to those sessions before advertising them as hosts or donors. No writes to installed mods or saved orders.

Galactic Conquest and campaign-linked tactical battles keep their native path. Network matches, Battlefront-specific controls and script-dependent abilities require separate compatibility work; do not enable them by lifting the solo guard. A future multiplayer handshake must agree on host, kits, rule versions and simulation digest on every peer before Ready.

## What earns a compatibility claim

| Gate | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| **Kit correctness** | Regression fixtures for the eight inspected Revan/Fall bodies: effective collision matches source. Negative controls for authored non-collidable units, an immunity, an unresolved weapon, and two different `Rebel` definitions. Missing critical data blocks launch, not native fallback. |
| **The first playable matchup** | From the [mounted Universal window](http://127.0.0.1:8590/), run **Revan Sith vs. Fall Trade Federation** on stock Abregado-rae in Skirmish/Instant × land/space. Reverse human and AI seats. In a seeded encounter with known vulnerable targets and legal attack orders, record a weapon event and positive shield/hull loss on a *mobile opponent* **in both directions**. A station hit, projectile count, health deficit without attacker identity, or just reaching the map is not sufficient. |
| **Each mode still plays** | In Skirmish both seats deploy, can build a unit of their chosen source, and the AI can spend and attack. In Instant the named products spawn for the right owners within budget; elimination produces a winner. Test two same-team seats of different sources plus an enemy; a spectator adds no army. |
| **Runtime parity** | Exercise both main-thread and worker space paths. Capture authoritative `{tick, attackerSeat, targetSeat, sourceUnit, targetUnit, weapon, actualShieldLoss, actualHullLoss}` events in test mode; export bounded counters and first positive examples, not per-shot traffic in normal play. Seeded reruns must agree on outcome and simulation state. Take a real firing screenshot per side with the corresponding tick and IDs. |
| **Isolation and recovery** | Save/reload mid-fight, return to a native battle and compare the native result to its control. Test stale kit, missing pack, same filenames with different bytes, reordered submods, and a failed second-seat import. No half-installed faction or overwritten native art. Repeat the nine native space Instant Battle and campaign-opening control cases from the prior QA run. |

**Scale beyond the showcase.** Generate the matrix from the installed catalog, not a hardcoded list of commanders. A read-only snapshot of the seven current sources advertised **63 land** and **69 space** away-roster entries. Counting distinct source/faction entries from *different* sources yields **1,643 land** and **1,971 space** unordered candidate pairs (`Σ nᵢnⱼ` for `i < j`); these are test targets, **not passes**. Run cheap deterministic encounters for all candidates in both modes and both seat directions. Run mounted UI tests for the reference matchup, each host, each exact saved playset, each domain and each execution path. Expand host-specific tests when a host changes; record the actual host/map rather than extrapolating to every map.

Publish results keyed by source revisions/order, faction pair, host, domain, mode and rules version. Distinguish **verified**, **failed**, **unsupported (reason)** and **not tested**; show both numerator and denominator. Don't claim “all factions” from one example per mod. Release mixed Instant Battle only after the reference fight passes all four mode/domain combinations and native controls stay green.
