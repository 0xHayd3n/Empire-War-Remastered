# Endor full landscape

In the **stock** profile of [Universal](http://127.0.0.1:8590/), open **Settings → Video → Graphics Quality → Endor full landscape**, then **Instant Battle → Endor → Land**. Start at a forest edge. Compare a ground-level view past the trees with the command view; switch the same setting off to restore the mounted art. This is a visual experiment for this specific map in both editions, not a global mod override. The original map still supplies layout, surface boundaries, slopes, weather, sun and fog. Units, structures, gameplay trees used as buildings, water, movement and collision are not part of the replacement.

### Quick play and comparison

With Universal running locally, [play Endor Ultra](http://127.0.0.1:8590/#endor-ultra) or [play the same battle with original assets](http://127.0.0.1:8590/#endor-original). Both links mount the stock profile and start an Imperial-versus-Rebel Endor land Instant Battle. They do not install a pack or save a graphics preset. Open the plain launcher to use your regular settings again. The original-assets link is **not** the 2006 executable: it uses mounted art in the same Universal renderer.

Ultra requests full 2K landscape materials, cinematic and receiver lighting, maximum shadow detail, bicubic texture filtering, and 2× supersampling subject to the engine's pixel budget. HDR is automatic on supported displays. The normal source and hardware checks still apply. `node tools/check-endor-ultra-link.cjs` opens both real launcher links in fresh Chrome contexts, waits for matching clear-noon weather, and captures the displayed frame at fixed cameras under the OS temp directory (`universal-endor-ultra`); it stages the same AT-ST for scale in the captures only. The player links retain the map’s normal weather roll and armies.

### Play through a shower

[Play Endor Showers](http://127.0.0.1:8590/#endor-showers) starts the Ultra battle in the stock **Noon_Rain** environment. The first **10 battle-seconds are clear**; let it run. Its own `RAIN_RANDOM` scenario brings lighter rain, a downpour and a break. Original and Ultra still roll a normal sky. Saved and network battles never take the shortcut.

At squad height, watch a sunlit patch on a trail: directional light and canopy shadows lose contrast as the cloud moves in. The forest catches rain: fewer drops make it through dense crowns, and scattered drips and damp bark outlast the rain. In shallow water, falling rain roughens the reflection with small rings. At command height the near-camera drops and extra light scattering fade away; the armies, HUD and fog of war remain unchanged. The feature samples the wide sun depth map along a short view ray, **not hardware ray tracing**. On Low texture detail the extra air samples are skipped. It adds no weather phases, wind or lightning to the simulation.

Toggle **Endor forest weather** under *Land battle lighting* for a live comparison. Off removes the forest-specific treatment; it does not stop the map’s weather. For the original sky and lighting as well, turn off [Immersive weather](immersive-weather.md). **Rain on terrain** also controls dampening on the new vegetation. Particle detail controls the near drops. The canopy and throughfall response requires the verified full Endor landscape.

`node tools/test-endor-weather.mjs` checks transitions and lighting restoration. `node tools/check-endor-weather.cjs` runs the mounted shortcut through clear, downpour and clearing, takes matched trail and command-HUD frames, verifies frozen weather and unit state across draws, and tries the toggle at 390px. Set `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` or `LOOK_EDITION=eaw` as needed. Captures go to `universal-endor-showers` in OS temp, not the repository.

### Tuning land battles

Under **Settings → Video → Graphics Quality → Land battle lighting**, *Unit highlight protection* rolls off bright lit models before they clip (not the HUD); *Shallow water clarity* mixes the map’s water treatment with the view through the shallows when Water depth and reflection is enabled. Those two apply to land battles generally. With full stock Endor enabled, *Endor canopy shade* controls the canopy’s light attenuation and *Endor distant haze* the added forest mist. The quick link uses 90% highlight protection, 36% water clarity, 85% canopy shade and 32% forest haze. Settings in quick play last for that launch; use the regular launcher to save personal choices.

**Foreground foliage fade** in the same panel opens a broad, soft-edged window through eligible plants in front of the tactical camera. It defaults to 70%; 0% disables the new effect. A fixed screen-space coverage pattern lines up through overlapping crowns so a unit stays visible even behind several layers. Ordinary alpha tests, depth writes and lighting still apply to the remaining leaves. The new fade is not applied to sun or reflection passes, first-person, chase or cinematic views. It does not change fog of war or reveal unseen units. Endor’s pre-existing narrow central cutaway still works at 0%. The Original comparison shortcut also leaves the new fade off.

The forest no longer runs the generic hard-surface gloss/rim-light shader on wood. It reads measured roughness and a repeating tangent normal. Trees, undergrowth and terrain receive direct sunlight from the same shadow maps as units. A static field supplies overhead sky cover and root contact, not a second cast shadow. The optional distant mist starts beyond the subject, not below a rising command camera. Whole-fir, bough, fern and shrub panels are [baked from CC0 models](../public/art/endor-thicket/SPRAYS.md); these are still cutouts, not individual 3D needles. The camera cutaway removes a whole obstructing tree rather than leaving a bare spike. Land contact shading ignores opaque depth *below* a transparent water sheet; dark edges from the submerged triangles should no longer be drawn on the water surface. These edits do not repaint the map, change fog of war or change the simulation.

### Daylight and cast shadows

The stock **Ground battlefield lighting** setting now measures the actual mounted skydome. It integrates incident light from its upper hemisphere in linear RGB, anchors exposure to the map’s three lights and keeps the two fill directions distinct. The underside of the painted dome does not light boots or vehicle bellies; that light comes from the terrain. Terrain, opaque units and trees use the same diffuse response. Ultra no longer adds an artificial sky rim to that light. An unavailable or black sky capture leaves the authored fallback in place. With water optics enabled, the sheet uses the sunlight and view distance **through water**, not camera height, to attenuate the bed; grazing views still reflect the bank.

On full Endor the sun draws a separate, bounded set of forest silhouettes using opaque trunks and the actual alpha-cut boughs, even when a tree is removed to clear the player's camera. Nearby ferns, bushes and young firs also cast. Beyond the bounded caster lists the forest retains overhead shelter, not a second set of projected crown shadows. Squad-height shadow resolution is fitted to the subject instead of the maximum shadow range, and comparison bias follows the size of a shadow texel. Two blocker-depth tests broaden nearby canopy penumbrae while short contact shadows stay tight. This is filtered shadow mapping and diffuse sky lighting, not path tracing. No scenery or shadow geometry enters the simulation.

`node tools/test-daylight-probe.mjs` checks the diffuse lobes and fallback. `node tools/check-ultra-daylight.cjs` captures the real Universal battle at fixed squad, command and trail cameras in **Noon_Clear** (OS temp `endor-irradiance`); `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` and `LOOK_EDITION=eaw` select fallbacks. `tools/check-ground-daylight.cjs` exercises stock Tatooine or `LOOK_WORLD=Hoth`, including lighting-off restoration. These are visual correctness checks, not sustained performance benchmarks.

### Ground

The complete mode takes precedence over the earlier **Endor scanned earth** setting. It never mixes the original terrain color, normals, gloss or baked terrain shading into the replacement material. Every used slot (including construction paving) must resolve to a material before the switch can apply. The names alone aren't an automatic mod rule: the stock profile, map and effective painted diffuse/bump files are checked. A changed or missing source keeps the scene on its own art. Painted transitions, fog of war, rainfall, shadowing and map-scale lighting continue to work. The ground-light bounce palette is rebuilt from the donor colors too.

| Endor surface | Replacement family |
| --- | --- |
| Temperate grass, forest grass | Greener woodland floor ([Ground 037](../public/art/endor-terrain/GRASS.md)) with separate grass meshes |
| Moss-covered ground, vegetated dirt/rock | Moss |
| Forest duff | Leaf litter |
| Bare dirt and paths | [Forest Ground 04](../public/art/endor-terrain/TRAIL.md): packed earth, pebbles and dry debris |
| Wet low ground and submerged terrain | Brown Mud 02; determined by the liquid water level, not a grass/moss paint label |
| Rock faces, including rotated cliff layers | Rock |
| Sandy shores | Sand |
| Construction surface (can be painted during play) | Concrete |

See the [natural material provenance](../public/art/endor-terrain/SOURCES.md) and [paving provenance](../public/art/endor-terrain/PAVING.md) for sources, licences and cook steps. The 2K library uses **two eight-layer BC3 texture arrays**, not the original 512px atlas and not sixteen independent shader samplers. With full mip chains, terrain adds **85.3 MiB at High**, **21.3 MiB at Medium**, or **5.3 MiB at Low**. Texture detail is read at first use; select a tier before starting a battle to compare. These are compressed GPU texture payloads, not total memory usage. The 2K source files are read locally even at the smaller tiers. The separate bare-earth-only option retains its dedicated 4K soil.

At close range the game replaces an 8×8-cell window of the visual ground with a subdivided mesh. Its actual vertices sample the corresponding donor heights, blended across the same painted boundaries. It meets the original large-scale ground geometry at its perimeter without changing the simulation height field. The patch has at most 18,432 triangles, is anchored to map cells and disappears at strategy distance. Per-pixel normals, parallax and short direct-light occlusion handle detail finer than the vertex spacing. Not hardware tessellation, ray tracing or a rebuild of pathfinding.

### Planting and edges

The full mode retains the original layout as a habitat map, not a count of how many plants may be drawn. In addition to replacing the authored plant props, it plants **five [CC0 3D models](../public/art/endor-grove/SOURCES.md)**: two kinds of meadow grass, woodland ferns, shrubs and young firs. They use the donor models’ actual UVs, geometry and photographed color. The mature authored tree placements use separately built trunk/branch/crown meshes, not the full-resolution Poly Haven mature-fir download. Buildings, including Ewok tree houses, stay original. The renderer checks stock Endor's participating scenery model and texture hashes, including two ALOs that differ between FoC and EaW.

Scatter is a deterministic function of map coordinates and live terrain paint. Grass clusters on meadow, ferns favor moss/litter, and saplings require woodland. Paths, paving, steep slopes, standing water and the areas immediately around build sites and capture points are excluded. Repainting a construction cell invalidates the local planting. These objects are render instances, never colliders or simulation entities. Up close, there are two independent grass silhouettes and a denser meadow tuft; individual blades are not drawn at command height. Medium and Low also reduce the number of additional plants.

Material transitions use a continuous world-space distortion of the paint weights, attenuated to zero normal to each shared cell edge, then read the neighboring **donor heights** to give raised details priority. This replaces the straight, bilinear crossfade in mixed cells only. Once draw slots are assigned, a material with zero weight cannot leak into the path blend. No noise is applied to a material’s hue and no stock diffuse is sampled for the transition.

### Ground at a trail, under shrubs, and at the waterline

Open meadow keeps the grass scan. Beneath shrubs and dense crowns the renderer selects moss and forest leaf litter from the same woodland that provides cover; near grass tufts favor openings. The original FoC map has only one dry vertex painted litter. The full landscape instead assigns litter to 24,006 sheltered grass/moss vertices. It leaves dry path, sand, rock and construction slots alone. Natural scans use locally rotated and offset coordinates blended across shared edges, with the same transforms for color, roughness, normal direction and height, including the displaced mesh. Regular paving retains its own alignment. No extra material arrays are allocated.

Submerged terrain is mud even where the authored map painted grass. The shore blends to the dry bank over a short height band. This band follows elevation on **both** sides of a cell boundary, including low dry cells. The close-up mesh uses the same band so its edges meet. Mud and dry materials are mixed in linear light before display encoding, avoiding a dark stripe halfway through the blend. Terrestrial foliage avoids wet footprints; wetland reeds and driftwood are not treated as grass. This affects draw instances and ground shading only. `node tools/test-endor-habitat.mjs` checks repeat correlation, seams, rectangular map indexing and untouched paint. `node tools/check-endor-habitat.cjs` captures a trail, understory, squads, command view, shoreline and the same lakebed with only the water sheets hidden. It writes to OS temp. The quick comparison uses the same pre-existing Endor links.

### Whole-map forest

The camera-local detailed plants are only the foreground. On the two reviewed stock Endor editions `endor-thicket.js` builds a deterministic population for the entire terrain at load. FoC's fixed plan adds **3,390 old-growth trees, 5,067 smaller conifers, 21,910 shrub groups, 40,167 fern groups, 226 downed logs and 1,551 stone groups**. These are placements, not simultaneous draw calls or all full-detail meshes. Base-game counts differ slightly. Woodland is not planted on open water, painted dirt corridors or paving; capture and build areas have reserved space. The sense of density comes from overlapping crowns, flared trunks, shoulder-height growth and banks of ferns, not just higher grass saturation. The largest trees remain visible at command zoom. The camera skips added foliage directly obstructing its line of sight to the focus. Both geometry level and instance caps limit rendering cost.

A map-space shelter texture is built from the actual tree locations. Changing sunlight no longer regenerates that texture or invalidates the grass-placement cache. The direct shadow and local light shafts instead sample GPU depth rendered from the sky’s sun direction. It is an approximation, not per-leaf ray tracing or volumetric sun shafts. The distant stand adds a modest cool haze; the references' structures, character art and cinematic lighting are not recreated. Visual-only planting means that extra scenery is not a collision or cover object. Map repainting rebuilds the planting and light field, never the other way around.

`node tools/test-endor-thicket.mjs` checks that the population spans all quadrants, respects open corridors and is deterministic after repaints. The mounted browser check visits two separated wooded trails plus the original clearing, saves fixed-camera pairs and checks that rendering reverts. Sustained combat and mobile GPUs have not been benchmarked.

**Panning costs.** Groundcover remembers both accepted and rejected world-space planting sites in bounded rings per species. Terrain paint and canopy texture changes invalidate the rings. Camera distance, ordering and the height of the moving detailed surface are still evaluated for the current view. `node tools/check-grove-scroll.cjs` measured 15,916 layer reads rather than 213,648 over 16 close pans; the visible matrices matched cold rebuilds exactly. The map-wide tree and shadow batches now publish only their live instances: in the mounted four-leg WebGL pan test, actual `bufferSubData` payload fell from 81.36 MB to 3.22 MB over 112 frames, with no uploads past the live prefixes. The direct WebGPU path already copied those prefixes.

`node tools/bench-ultra-pan.cjs` runs a live 1280×720 pan. For the pre-change comparison set `LOOK_FOREST_REFERENCE_DIR` to a directory holding the previous `endor-grove.js` and `endor-thicket.js` (also disables the new camera fade in that run). Two local before/after pairs in opposite orders gave median WebGL frame intervals of 38.82→33.30 and 50.03→44.36 ms; p95 49.94→39.03 and 55.75→50.12 ms. The WebGPU median remained ~5.6 ms and p95 ~11.1 ms. These are measurements of this route, not sustained-combat or mobile FPS results.

### Verification

`node tools/check-canopy-view.cjs` compares the same frozen forest and tactical views on the mounted shortcut at 0%, 40% and 80%. It checks that the fade extends beyond the central cutaway, leaves the corners alone, resets cleanly and reveals a real walker behind a stand. `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` checks fallback; captures go to OS temp `canopy-view`. `tools/check-endor-ultra-link.cjs` also checks keyboard adjustment in the 390px settings layout.

**Hairline sky leaks at close zoom.** The coarse terrain now removes whole triangles where the detailed patch replaces them, rather than discarding fragments by their interpolated world coordinates. Each patch perimeter segment shares the same two endpoints as its coarse neighbour: subdivision fans meet the original edge instead of leaving raster T-junctions. The outermost subtriangle strip stays on the coarse plane, so ground cover still samples the drawn surface. No skirts, overlaid ground, changes to map holes, or gameplay height changes. `node tools/check-land-sky-cracks.cjs` alternates black and bright green backdrops behind three frozen mounted ground views. The complete ground should be unchanged; deliberately hiding the replacement must expose the backdrop. `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` runs the fallback. Captures stay under `land-sky-cracks` in OS temp.

A separate grid could be visible **inside a single scan material**, even with no terrain crack: blending rotated patches averaged away fine colour and surface-normal detail in evenly spaced strips. The scan shader now compensates for that loss of contrast around the donor's measured mean colour. It leaves height, roughness, paint and paving alignment alone and fades out the correction when zoomed far enough away that the grain is no longer resolved. `node tools/check-endor-tile-lines.cjs` measures grain and average brightness at joins versus patch centres in the real lakebed and dry leaf litter (`LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` for fallback). Captures go to the OS temp directory `endor-tile-lines`.

For close-up seams, `node tools/test-endor-bank.mjs` compares the duplicated vertices of adjoining wet and dry cells. `node tools/test-endor-habitat.mjs` also checks the slope across the scan patches, not just their height. `node tools/check-terrain-shade.cjs` exercises the mounted terrain's WebGL shader under a directional sky with no direct sun: stock and scanned materials should both respond to opposing normals, and mid-bank pixels should agree across the tile edge. The mounted habitat check includes a `low-bank` view for both renderers.

`node tools/test-endor-landscape.mjs` verifies every packaged material and mip chain. `node tools/test-endor-relief.mjs` checks that the local mesh really moves vertically, its boundary meets the coarse terrain and the simulation heights don't move. `node tools/check-endor-landscape.cjs` opens the mounted Universal launcher with Chrome/Playwright, checks the toggle with keyboard at narrow width, enters a battle, and captures fixed-camera off/on/off views. Set `LOOK_EDITION=eaw` for the base game, `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` for fallback, or `LANDSCAPE_FAIL=1` to corrupt the material index and verify that the whole scene stays stock. `LANDSCAPE_MIP=1` or `2` tests the smaller tiers. The fixed-frame check also renders the new terrain once with its old linear blend, holding lighting, camera, donors and plants constant, and checks that the transition changes. `node tools/test-endor-grove.mjs` checks the packed 3D plant geometry and habitat exclusions. Private shots and JSON are written to `universal-endor-landscape` in the OS temp directory, not the repo.
