# Lighting a whole ground battlefield

Open [mounted Universal](http://127.0.0.1:8590/), start a land battle, and use the normal strategy camera. In **Settings → Video → Graphics Quality → World environments**, toggle **Ground battlefield lighting**. Use **Battle realism → Use individual settings** to compare only this change; **On** enables it automatically. The individual default is off. No model replacement or texture pack is required.

On Tatooine, look at the shaded side of a vehicle next to the sand. On Endor, pan along the tree line. The sun is blocked by a caster, but the indirect light is not: the upper surfaces see more of the map’s sky/fill light, while downward-facing surfaces pick up diffuse color from the ground. The same calculation lights painted terrain and ordinary lit models, including infantry, vehicles, buildings and props. The ground color is an area-weighted estimate from small mips of the map’s mounted diffuse layers, not a sample of the screen. A missing readable mip uses a neutral fallback. It is map-wide indirect lighting, not local interreflection, and it doesn’t change emissive effects, fog visibility, stats or orders.

With Shadows enabled, strategy pans use a world-aligned shadow grid. At medium and higher shadow resolution, a smaller, finer map follows the camera target; its edges blend into the wider, less frequently updated map. Both draw existing casters, not duplicate units. Soft, short ground-contact patches help anchor visible grounded units and foundations. Lowest shadow resolution avoids the second map. With Shadows off you retain the map fill without the new depth passes. First-person and cinematic cameras keep their own shadow framing.

**Remastered maps:** While the Endor, Yavin, Hoth or Tatooine landscape is active, both shadow maps follow the current rendered pose and the authored sun direction. The close map compares each filtered sample at the depth of the receiving triangle, so a slope doesn't shadow itself in stripes. Foliage cutouts cast with **Shadows** even without optional leaf backlighting. Units cast onto the rendered relief instead of also drawing a flat contact patch at the simulation height. Switching back to the original landscape restores its original shadow treatment without restarting the battle.

`node tools/check-land-shadow-footprints.cjs` measures a moving solid and a grouped leaf/bark caster on sloping terrain and model surfaces in the mounted remaster. `LOOK_WORLD` selects `endor`, `yavin`, `hoth` or `tatooine`; `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` selects the fallback. The check includes leaves just above the material's alpha cutoff, transparent gaps, low sun, self-shadowing, master Shadows off and switching the map treatment off and back on. With zero solar radiance, introducing a caster must not darken the terrain's ambient light, even without the optional daylight setting.

`node tools/check-land-unit-shadow.cjs` spawns a mounted AT-ST on Endor and measures its footprint on terrain, using the normal unit batch with other forest casters isolated. Reversing the sun along world X must move the shadow centroid to the other side; removing the unit must restore the ground pixels. Run with `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` to check the fallback.

`node tools/check-forest-shadow-work.cjs` checks the mounted Endor sky and both shadow cameras against three sun directions, including a grazing sun. The habitat texture must remain unchanged; orbiting at a fixed focus must not repack sun-view forest instances; disabling shadows must empty that list and re-enabling must repopulate it. Moving units still refresh the depth maps. These are counts of avoided CPU work, not a whole-battle FPS benchmark.

## Checks

**Broad black border on otherwise visible ground.** Separate the TED vertex shade, terrain texture and visibility channel before attributing it to water. On Yavin the black strip tracked the vertex shade on dry, fully visible terrain. Baked shade now attenuates diffuse fill without multiplying sunlight, fog or the whole final color. `DAY_WATER_AUDIT=1 node tools/check-ultra-daylight.cjs` compares a visible Endor shoreline with both sheets, each sheet separately, and the uncovered bed; repeat with `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl`. `DAY_ORIGINAL=1` runs the same camera and pass isolation on the original-art profile. Check all four images: pixels that stay black on both the bed and the surface would be missed by a difference mask alone.

**Distant scenery and natural finishes.** On active remasters, retained map props are culled by the camera frustum, not the tactical focus radius. Projected size selects the mesh; a large cliff can stay detailed at a distance where individual trees and rock clusters use much cheaper geometry. Compatible authored LODs take priority. Rigid meshes without them can share index-only reductions generated off the render thread; unsupported geometry keeps its original mesh rather than disappearing. Endor's replacement plants retain their far models in view, and its added old-growth landmarks use the existing bounded instance pools; fine grass and forest-floor scatter still have local budgets.

Stone and vegetation use broad, low highlights. Map sunlight, shadow, sky fill and soil light remain active; the existing rain-surface setting controls wetting. Only eligible map-prop material slots change, not units, water, glass, emissive fittings or the shared source material. New scenery is classified automatically: dedicated tree/grass shaders, natural texture names (including stone families and leaf atlases) and prop/model names identify eligible slots. No XML entry or per-map model list is required. Generic materials with no natural cues and manufactured or special-effect slots keep their authored appearance. `Scenery_Surface` remains available for ambiguous assets; `authored` explicitly disables inference. Changing back to the original landscape restores the actual source materials and geometry.

`tools/check-scenery-distance.cjs` checks the mounted rock, tree and cliff batches at multiple distances and behind the camera. `tools/check-scenery-finish.cjs` isolates a mounted Tatooine rock cluster in the live lighting pass, compares its highlight at two camera angles with its stock material, checks wetting and toggles the landscape. Run each with Node and then with `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl`.

**Ink lines on rocks.** Close, oblique Tatooine views exposed a different failure from the detached shadows: the screen-space contact pass treated depth folds *within* opaque cliffs as creases, then darkened the already fogged colour. On remasters, only the terrain side of a contact receives this pass, and its contribution fades when the footprint is too small to resolve. Real surface lighting, cast shadows, image sharpening and material detail are unaffected. `node tools/check-scenery-ink.cjs` compares the same near and distant ledges with the live pass, no contact, and the old receiver rule; `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` checks the fallback.

**Repeated silhouettes around rocks and infantry.** The contact pass was sampling up to 28 texels away, so the outline of a small rock or boot could appear several times around it, independently of its sun shadow. On an active remaster, contact shading now samples only immediate neighbours (about 2.5 display pixels) and darkens creases gently. Directional shadows still come from the cast maps. `node tools/check-remaster-shadow-halos.cjs` holds a mounted Tatooine rock cluster and deployed squad still, isolates their visible geometry, and checks for contact darkening on open ground while confirming that the longer sun shadow remains. Set `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` to check the fallback.

**Dark lines on cliffs and banks.** Contact shading must not trace the triangles of a bare hillside. In the main land colour pass, terrain marks its coverage in the scene target's alpha channel. When the receiving pixel is terrain, only non-terrain coverage at a depth sample contributes contact shade. This uses the existing colour target; it does not blur the rock, flatten the relief, or change cast shadows. `node tools/check-endor-cliff-contact.cjs` compares contact strength on/off at three mounted Endor Ultra shorelines, first in the complete scene and then with just the ground. The bare-ground difference should almost disappear; the populated scene should still show contact shading. Use `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` for the fallback. Captures go to the OS temp directory `endor-cliff-contact`.

**Map edges and reflective surfaces.** A planar slope keeps the same lighting normal up to the map perimeter. Water, ice and lava use the wet part of each terrain triangle rather than two complete triangles for a partly wet tile. Turning a flow layer rotates both texture axes. A hidden or submerged water pass does not consume the first reflection update when the camera comes back above water. These are rendering changes, not changes to movement or water level.

`node tools/test-land-surface-pass.mjs` checks the geometry and reflection state, including an interrupted draw. `node tools/check-land-liquid.cjs` opens mounted Endor Ultra, checks the actual shoreline mesh, and renders a two-axis calibration texture through the liquid shader at 0° and 90° (`LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` for fallback). For close-range terrain rebuilds, `node tools/test-endor-relief.mjs` checks that repeated corner slots share a height sample and distinct rotated slots do not. Set `RELIEF_BENCH=1` for a local CPU-only rebuild timing; it does not measure battle FPS.

**Ultra terrain submission and scrolling.** The main-thread land renderers keep the original coarse mesh in one draw. A two-level visibility test selects a conservative contiguous range for each camera, including the reflection camera. Small maps and the optional render worker retain the full draw. At close zoom the displaced patch caches untapered donor heights for at most 8×8 world cells (~85 KiB); newly exposed cells are sampled normally, repaint invalidates the cache, and the patch edge is tapered and stitched at its current position. No shorter draw distance, lower resolution, or change to gameplay heights.

`node tools/test-terrain-visibility.mjs` checks every possibly visible triangle against the range. `node tools/test-relief-scroll.mjs` compares the scrolling result against a freshly rebuilt mesh, including vertices, shading, stitching, holes, and repaint. `node tools/check-ultra-terrain-cost.cjs` compares full and trimmed draws at three fixed views through mounted Endor Ultra (`LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` for fallback). At 1280×720, coarse main triangles were 14,292 (close), 53,876 (tactical), and 177,320 (overview), versus 192,200 without culling. No visible pixel differences passed the comparison threshold. These are submission counts, not FPS.

`node tools/bench-ultra-pan.cjs` times a warmed 300-frame close pan while the battle simulation runs. For a pre-change comparison, use `LOOK_FULL_TERRAIN=1 LOOK_RELIEF_REFERENCE=<saved pre-change endor-relief.js>` in a separate run. Two run pairs in opposite order on this machine saw median total JS frame time **on terrain-rebuild frames** fall from 8.86–14.58 to 4.64–5.55 ms on WebGPU, and from 12.08–16.57 to 6.94–7.22 ms on WebGL. Whole-frame display intervals varied between runs; this is evidence of reduced CPU spikes, not a guaranteed average FPS increase. Reports and verification captures go under OS temp `ultra-terrain-cost`.

**Shared Endor / Yavin close terrain.** An 8×8-cell ring now keeps interpolated normals (127 KiB), plus authored shade where used (42 KiB), alongside untapered heights. A one-cell scroll copies lighting for the 56 overlapping cells; the exposed eight cells are sampled normally. Heights at the moving rim, holes, stitching and current draw slots are still evaluated. The CPU sampler no longer allocates an array of three transformed donors for each vertex or reads a zero-weight donor. A repaint or a change to source normals/shade invalidates the stored data.

`node tools/check-ultra-relief-cost.cjs` exercises the real maps; set `LOOK_SHARED_REFERENCE_DIR` to a folder with the pre-change `endor-relief.js` and `endor-tiles.js` for a byte-for-byte A/B and isolated timings. Across 48 moves on each mounted map, all six drawn geometry streams agreed on both WebGPU and WebGL. Alternating 42-move batches at close zoom measured 1.12→0.76 ms and 1.35→0.85 ms per Endor rebuild on WebGPU and WebGL respectively, and 1.21→1.00 ms and 1.19→0.97 ms on Yavin. These measure CPU rebuild time, not whole-frame FPS. The live pan tool also supports `LOOK_WORLD=yavin`; include the old `land-sky-light.js` in the reference directory for that A/B.

**Reflection warm-up.** The 48px-per-face sky atlas still uses the same 40-direction kernels and three roughness levels. On land, a short-lived worker now filters it after readback instead of running ~1.1 million lookups on the render thread when a new sky becomes ready. `tools/test-land-filter-job.mjs` checks byte parity, cancellation on sky change and fallback when workers are blocked. `tools/test-land-reflection-readback.mjs` checks readback retries.

**Forest-to-water pan.** `node tools/check-endor-pan-cost.cjs` opens mounted Endor Ultra and moves the close camera along four fixed paths (also run with `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl`). An independent check against the drawn liquid triangles identifies the dry views. Before region culling, WebGPU drew 28 unnecessary mirrored scenes in the 56 dry frames. Afterward both backends draw none on that route; the other 56 frames still get 28 reflection updates. This is conservative visibility, not occlusion culling: a small region that partly intersects the view may still request a reflection. Water and ice keep a single sheet and mirror target, and the first visible draw on a different renderer refreshes its own copy.

On the same 112-frame route, 40 close-patch rebuilds used to invalidate 197,120 bytes of identical triangle indices and local corner coordinates apiece. Both arrays stay resident on that route. Holes shorten the draw range; only a changed edge-stitch pattern in a packed slot can dirty the indices. Position, normal, layer, and shade updates continue. These are counts of draws and invalidated attributes, not a battle FPS or GPU bandwidth benchmark (WebGPU packs vertex attributes together).

`node tools/test-ground-daylight.mjs` checks painted-layer weighting, absent textures, finer near-map spacing, and stationary world points under a sub-texel camera pan. `node tools/check-ground-daylight.cjs` checks the real setting (including persistence and narrow keyboard use), then launches a populated mounted land Instant Battle. `LOOK_WORLD` selects the planet, `LOOK_PROFILE` its installed library id, and `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` the fallback. The stock Tatooine fixture adds one mounted AT-ST and one completed barracks to the ordinary infantry force. Endor and the mod battlefield use their starting scenes unchanged. The test isolates terrain and object pixels independently, checks that sun occlusion doesn't black out the sampled shadows, tests cast-shadows-off, and switches back to the original frame. It writes its images and reports under the OS temporary directory `universal-ground-daylight`, not into the repository.

Tested at 1920×1080 on stock `_MP_LAND_TATOOINE.TED`, stock `_MP_LAND_ENDOR.TED`, and the installed Kuat `_MOD_LAND_VIC_KUAT_SPACE_STATION.ted` (profile `1125571106`), on both main-thread WebGPU and WebGL. The short matched render pans had similar median intervals off/on on this machine; Kuat WebGL’s 95th percentile rose from roughly 5.7 to 11.1 ms. These are warm, fixed-simulation comparisons, not mobile, worker-rendering or sustained combat benchmarks. Water, changing weather, global illumination and temporal reconstruction are separate work.
