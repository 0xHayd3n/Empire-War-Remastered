**Within the snowfield:** a featureless flat previously used the same 90:10 wind-snow/firn mix at every sampled point. Irregular bands of wind-scoured firn now break up that sheet without revealing bedrock on the flats. In the mounted eastern deployment and rib basin, the p10–90 range of the loose-snow share was 43 and 41 percentage points respectively. These are material weights, not a change in brightness. The original high-frequency scans, their measured scale and authored lighting stay in place.

The three TED environments are `Sunrise_Snow`, `Noon_Snow` and `Nighttime_Snow`. All use `SNOW_RANDOM`. A strong snow phase and a naturally occurring clear interval are separate observations; switching the visual environment during a frozen comparison does not set weather strength. The ground checker holds only diffuse RGB constant to distinguish material hue from real sky and sun color. A separate normal-only diagnostic isolated a quilted command-height pattern: the two snow captures had tilted average tangent normals. The opt-in cook removes that mean slope before packing, not the local slopes, contrast or grain.
