# A playable Star Destroyer material comparison

In the mounted [Universal window](http://127.0.0.1:8590/), open **Settings → Video → Graphics Quality → Space battles → Star Destroyer surfaces**. Original is the default. **Paint and alloy** keeps the map’s sun and fill, shades the painted hull as a rough dielectric, and gives the equatorial trench and nozzle collars a less diffuse, smoother alloy response. **Paint and alloy · orbital light** uses exactly those surfaces and adds diffuse sky light and roughness-filtered reflections from the battlefield’s environment. On a dark map the orbital difference should be subtle, not a universal blue tint.

In a stock space battle, select a single Star Destroyer. Right-drag from the selected ship toward a new heading and release to turn it in place. Compare the lit deck and shadow-facing plates at strategy zoom, then zoom in toward the stern. The original engine emitters stay independently lit; the existing optional close-up fittings and bridge windows remain a separate feature. Switching surfaces does not select a global space look or alter orders, damage, geometry, team paint or mounted files.

The renderer checks both the stock hull geometry fingerprint and the expected opaque bump/colorize material and texture name. Material zones are defined in hull coordinates; gloss and normals still come from the mounted art. The response combines direct light, fill, nearby blast light and the optional orbital capture before the opaque hull’s output transform. It reuses the existing six-direction diffuse probe and the three-band reflection atlas (one capture per environment/look, not per ship). If the atlas is not ready, the paint-and-alloy treatment still works. This is not a new high-resolution asset, a scene-wide linear compositor, metalness inferred from texture brightness, or ship-to-ship ray tracing.

## Check

With Chrome, `playwright-core` and the mounted stock game available: `node tools/check-hull-surfaces.cjs`; repeat with `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl`. The script also exercises the narrow keyboard-accessible setting and persistence. PNGs and the numeric report are written only to the OS temporary directory under `universal-hull-surfaces` (`LOOK_SHOTS` overrides the root).

The mounted check issued a normal facing order in stock Abregado Rae with a modeled planet, 100 roster entries per side, 1920×1080. At two poses of that turn it held ship, camera, shadows and exposure still and captured all three settings at fleet and close zoom. With the planet visible near the stern in the close view, the existing engine sprites did not turn into painted highlights. At fleet zoom the paint change affected more than 20,000 pixels by at least six summed RGB code values in each comparison; adding orbital light affected at least 3,900 more by that measure. Returning to Original gave an exact pixel match at both distances on both backends. Hiding the subject produced zero changed pixels elsewhere.

A short live panning sample (64 browser frame intervals per setting; four alternating windows) measured median / p95 times of **5.56 / 5.57 ms Original, 5.56 / 5.59 ms orbital** on WebGPU and **16.74 / 22.42 ms Original, 16.80 / 33.35 ms orbital** on WebGL. Those are browser frame intervals, not isolated GPU costs or a portable FPS promise. Other maps, HDR displays, mobile GPUs and overhaul-specific art were not benchmarked.
