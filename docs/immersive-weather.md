# Immersive land weather

**Try it:** [Endor, clear noon into rain](http://127.0.0.1:8590/#endor-showers) · [Clash on Tatooine, blowing sand and clear intervals](http://127.0.0.1:8590/#tatooine-remaster) · [Yavin Training Grounds](http://127.0.0.1:8590/#yavin-remaster). At squad height, compare the open sky, the sunlit side of a soldier and the shadow under their feet. The effect changes the light falling on the battlefield, not the HUD or a full-screen color filter.

The sky is drawn from a camera-centred cube, with no stock sky model, painted sky texture, or downloaded art. The same sky appears from ground level, at command zoom, and in planar water reflections.

**What to watch for:** In an open patch of sky on Endor, fair-weather cloud towers and high wisps give way to rain curtains when showers arrive. At sunrise on a wooded map, look for bars of cloud or low mist near the horizon. On Hoth, thin ice clouds can form a halo around the daytime sun; on a clear polar night, stars can give way to a green auroral curtain. Tatooine instead gets pale, wind-stretched dust beneath a diffuse sun. These are possibilities within a forecast, not events every battle must play.

In Universal, open **Settings → Video → Graphics Quality → Land battle lighting → Immersive weather**:

- **All outdoor land maps** (new default): stock, remastered, and Workshop land battles get a generated sky, even if the map has no usable dome. Existing saved scope choices are respected.
- **Remastered maps**: only while a reviewed stock landscape is active. **Stock land maps**: outdoor stock maps, even without a terrain remaster. Try Hoth for snow, Polus for a blizzard, or Shola for ash.
- **Original sky**: load and restore the mounted backdrop, light and haze in the same battle. This switch does not disable precipitation or the separate **Endor forest weather** treatment for wet trees, drips and water rings.

| Forecast | Battlefield look |
| --- | --- |
| Clear daylight | Deep blue overhead, pale horizon, hard warm key light and cool, legible shadows. Sunrise and sunset use a warm horizon instead; a clear night shows stars, which fade behind the incoming weather. |
| Rain | Slate cloud banks, soft cool fill and a subdued sun. The existing liquid-rain system still controls wet surfaces and drying. |
| Snow | Pale blue-grey sky and reflected light, with more distance and more direct light than a blizzard. |
| Blizzard | Low-contrast white sky, wind-stretched cloud and reduced distance. Uses the blizzard forecast, not every snow phase. |
| Sandstorm | Pale mineral dust softens distant ridges and diffuses the sun. Windblown wisps pick up the horizon light; nearby sand and armour keep their material colors. A clear phase opens the view again. |
| Ash | Charcoal cloud with a muted copper horizon; does not wet the ground. |

The [24 formations](../public/js/sky-formations.js) vary cloud height, shape, spacing, and near-horizon effects. Each climate and phase uses a subset. Even during a long clear interval, the pattern gradually changes over two to three minutes of **battle time**. Pausing freezes it, and switching back from **Original sky** picks it up at the current battle time rather than starting over.

**For map authors:** the [shared presets and resolver](../public/js/immersive-weather.js) follow the mounted phase name, continuous strength and ease-out endpoints. `CLEAR`, `RAIN`, `SNOW`, `SANDSTORM` and `ASH` are the stock phase types; Blizzard is a stock scenario using `SNOW`. The [sky shader](../public/js/land-atmosphere.js) draws the horizon, sun, cloud banks and stars from view direction. Cloud placement is seeded by the world and scrolls with the map’s wind on the battle clock. The map supplies the sun direction and horizon tint; the forecast drives both the atmosphere and the temporary lighting on the ground. Precipitation, sound and lightning remain map-owned. Filtered material reflections sample the selected sky after warm-up and refresh on significant weather changes rather than every frame. No new forecast, save field or multiplayer input is introduced.

**Frame budget:** The generated sky stays one draw. Downward non-dust views skip cloud noise below the horizon; absent stars, rings and auroras cost no effect evaluations. The visible sky and planar water still follow every forecast sample. Filtered material lighting waits for short transitions to settle; long custom fades get rate-limited updates. Diffuse and material probes share an in-flight GPU readback when they need the same capture. Filtering runs off-thread, or in cancellable, short slices if a worker is unavailable. These changes don't reduce particle counts or lower resolution.

No per-map registration is needed. Sunset, volcanic, thin-atmosphere and ringed environments use their map cues. Custom haze, mist, fog, drizzle, downpour, thunderstorm, flurries and sleet phases have distinct light and visibility recipes. Light rain/snow strengths and lightning-bearing rain also select lighter or stormier looks without adding precipitation or flashes. An unknown phase keeps the local base sky rather than inventing weather. Explicit interiors and cinematic environments retain their authored backdrops. Space battles are unchanged. Fog that the map or player disabled stays disabled.

**Verification:** `npm run test:immersive-weather` checks phase families, formation reachability, clock determinism, easing, night, binding scope and exact light restoration. With Universal running, Chrome and resolvable `playwright-core`, `node tools/check-immersive-weather.cjs` captures matched HUD-on frames on the maps above and checks the in-battle setting at narrow width. Set `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` for the second renderer, or `LOOK_WORLDS=Tatooine,Polus` for a subset. Set `LOOK_FORMATIONS=1` to read back the generated sky in the mounted battle: holding the camera, lighting, phase and wind offset fixed, advancing only the formation clock must change pixels and restoring it must reproduce them exactly. For a local frame-cost comparison, run `LOOK_COST=1 LOOK_WORLDS=Tatooine node tools/check-immersive-weather.cjs` (set `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl` for WebGL). It alternates warmed Original/generated draws in the same frozen scene and reports median and p95 completed milliseconds for the sky pass and whole frame. Both modes retain the map’s particles. This is a rendering comparison, not a live FPS or minimum hardware guarantee. `LOOK_SKY_MS` optionally enforces a local median sky-pass budget; no instrumentation runs in the game. Shots stay in OS temp under `universal-immersive-weather`. `node tools/check-procedural-land-sky.cjs` launches Workshop Dantooine on the same board, verifies the default without a stock sky load, removes dome metadata in the isolated test session, then checks original and restored modes. The matched weather views select a mounted environment, wait for the requested phase and freeze the battle for the comparison; `node tools/check-endor-weather.cjs` separately follows an uninterrupted Endor forecast through rain and clearing.
