# Compare an explosion beside the flagship

In the [mounted Universal window](http://127.0.0.1:8590/), start a stock Abregado Rae space Instant Battle with a Star Destroyer and wait for it to finish arriving. In **Settings → Video → Graphics Quality**, choose **Use individual settings** and switch **Space battles → Linear battle lighting** on. **Battle realism: On** also enables it, along with other settings. For an isolated comparison, keep those other settings fixed. Leave **Explosion light on hulls** on and choose **World environments → Orbital lighting → Sunlit atmosphere + planet light** to see both light sources together.

Click **Preview explosion lighting** under **All battles**. It pans past the flagship and triggers one visual-only flash; it does not deal damage or issue an order. Look at the plates next to the flash, then at the engines and the planet’s dark side. Escape ends the pan. The existing **Pan past the planet** is still available without the flash. Repeat with linear lighting off for the original renderer. The camera route and flash position are repeatable relative to the ship, not a rewind of the ongoing battle or an identical random particle pattern.

## Rendering contract

Opaque hulls light their paint in linear RGB. Sun, existing planet bounce, and local blast energy combine before output. Archive materials, including planets and additive engines/particles, retain their authored textures and shaders; a scene-only adapter converts their display RGB to linear **before** blending. Alpha stays alpha. Values above one are treated as extended sRGB signal rather than clamped. Archive tint, fog, and sprite fades aren't reauthored. This is not a physical emitter model or path tracing.

The scene, any supersample resolve, and bloom buffers use half-float color. The bloom pass extracts bright energy without clipping and adds the blurred result before a single SDR highlight roll-off. Extended WebGPU output keeps headroom for the existing HDR presenter/calibration. Heat displacement, shadow maps, blend factors, reflection probes and DOM HUD stay outside the conversion. Land, menus and unsupported renderers retain their earlier path. The individual setting defaults off. The larger targets cost memory/bandwidth; use the original path if a device struggles.

## Evidence

- `node tools/test-scene-light.mjs` checks color vs factor/mask handling, shader entry points and settings fallback.
- `node tools/check-scene-light.cjs` uses the mounted window, a saved keyboard setting and the preview button. Run again with `LOOK_BACKEND=webgl`. It starts a 100-entry-per-side fleet, freezes **one** preview explosion, replays three camera poses, and reads the actual battle canvas. A separate two-draw floating-target test produced **1.2070 red / 0.4316 green** on both backends (expected 1.2077 / 0.4317). Merely brightening an 8-bit picture cannot pass this test.
- In the matched 1080p captures, thousands of previously near-white ship pixels regained color. The hull-light toggle lifted facing plates and left thousands of other ship pixels effectively unchanged. The sampled night-side brightness did not change from that local light; nozzle cores stayed bright and green markings retained their color. A visible HUD element kept its layout, color and text. Screenshots and reports are private temporary files under `universal-scene-light`.
- Warm matched-pan median intervals were about 5.6 ms on WebGPU and 16.6 ms on WebGL, both off and on. In the recorded WebGL run the 95th percentile rose from 16.7 to 22.1 ms. This was one machine, a fixed explosion and a short pan, not a sustained-combat or mobile benchmark.
- `HDR_SCENE_LIGHT=1 node tools/check-universal-hdr.cjs` checked actual extended WebGPU output and the return to SDR. Set `HDR_SHOTS` to a temporary directory for private captures. The test machine did not report an HDR display; the test emulated the display capability signal, not the shader or render targets. Physical HDR scan-out was not tested.
