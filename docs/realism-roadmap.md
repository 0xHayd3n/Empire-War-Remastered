# A reference shot before a renderer rewrite

**Decision:** keep Universal's WebGPU/WebGL battle renderer. The [first playable flyby](destroyer-flyby.md) adds generated close-up geometry to the mounted stock asset; the full replacement asset and scene-linear pipeline below remain proposals. See also the [native rendering feasibility appendix](native-rendering-feasibility.md). Build one near-camera, physically based Star Destroyer and compare it against today's Deep space ship and an offline reference. Use that result to decide where to spend the next art or rendering effort. Photographic close-ups, readable moving fleets and ground combat have different limiting factors.

**Fleet-camera milestone:** [Explosion light on hulls](fleet-blast-light.md) now lights nearby ships in playable space battles on WebGPU and WebGL. The comparison isolates the flash on a turning stock Star Destroyer at strategy zoom; it isn't a renderer rewrite or a new asset pack.

**Motion milestone:** [Steady fleet edges](fleet-edges.md) is a bounded, optional spatial alternative to temporal AA. It was checked against matched subpixel pans, fighter contrast, first-frame disappearance and live frame intervals on both backends.

**Material milestone:** [Star Destroyer surfaces](hull-surfaces.md) compares the same playable hull under original shading, a linear paint/alloy response and captured orbital light. This changes the stock opaque hull, not the geometry or the whole effects compositor.

**Damage milestone:** [A shielded strike, exposed aft-plate strike and lost engine bank](hull-battle-damage.md) now read differently on the playable stock Star Destroyer. Scorch marks remain on the hit surface as the ship turns.

**Environment milestone:** [Orbital lighting over Abregado Rae](orbital-lighting.md) compares the same fleet pan in the existing environment, with a sunlit atmospheric limb, and with reflected planet light on the hull. The comparison does not change the map sun or camera exposure.

**Lighting pipeline milestone:** [Linear battle lighting](linear-battle-lighting.md) now combines space hull light, archive FX and bloom before the output transform, with a repeatable explosion preview. Legacy material inputs and display-space tint/fog remain supported; this is not a rewrite of every lighting equation.

**Land battlefield milestone:** [Ground battlefield lighting](ground-battlefield-lighting.md) shares sun, sky fill and terrain bounce across lit units and ground without a model refit. The camera-focused and wide shadow maps were checked on two stock maps and one installed mod map.

**Land weather milestone:** [Rain on terrain](rain-on-terrain.md) gives soil, vegetation and paving different responses to rain, and thins authored movement dust while the ground dries. Dry or non-rain maps keep their original appearance.

**Depth and motion milestone:** [Aerial perspective, shared wind and steady surface detail](battlefield-depth-motion.md) add height-dependent land haze, link vegetation and particles to the moving cloud shadows, and reduce texture shimmer during strategy-camera pans. The surface filter has no history; temporal reconstruction remains a separate option for future work.

## Next renderer-wide opportunities after depth, rain and wind (proposals)

The six proposals below are retained as design rationale. Their first playable implementations and measured limits are documented in [Battlefield light and materials](field-receivers.md). The separate land linear-light pipeline remains a follow-up.

1. **Moving cloud shadows on the army.** [Terrain](../public/js/game/terrain.js) samples the moving map cloud texture; the [ordinary model shader](../public/js/model.js) does not sample that same field. Reuse its world coordinates and phase when lighting units, props and buildings. Attenuate *direct sun*, not the framebuffer: sky fill, team markings and emissive windows should not disappear. Do not apply the cloud term twice to the ground. On Endor, hold the camera fixed and watch one cloud boundary cross a walker and the road under it. Include an untextured sky as a no-change control. This is the recommended first pass: no new cloud volume or per-unit shadow rendering.
2. **Directional light on smoke and dust.** Keep the authored puffs, but let sunlight give a drifting cloud shape and let a nearby flash light it locally. [Particle feasibility and acceptance](lit-particles-feasibility.md) covers particle classification, the existing blast field and the soft-depth difference between backends. A small per-sprite lighting test comes before expensive volumes.
3. **Roughness-dependent reflections across fleets and vehicles.** [Existing finish probes](../public/js/game/orbitprobe.js) and the [three-band filter](../public/js/finishatlas.js) already separate sharp and broad sky reflections on selected hulls/fittings. Broaden the receivers to compatible materials on ordinary ships, vehicles and structures. Use one capture per environment, not one per unit. Replace the old specular approximation rather than adding a second reflection. Never infer bare metal from a team-color, emissive or opacity mask; ambiguous mod art stays matte. Compare a cruiser, a fighter and a ground vehicle through a turn. [Filament's material/IBL reference](https://google.github.io/filament/main/filament.html) explains the distinction between roughness and metallicity.
4. **Light through leaves.** Add subtle backlighting to foliage where the sun shines through and stable clumps of dappled shadow on nearby receivers. [Model material/depth behavior](../public/js/model.js), [land caster selection](../public/js/game/landgame.js) and the [instancer](../public/js/game/landinstances.js) matter more than extra geometry. A read-only inventory of `_MP_LAND_ENDOR.TED` found 1,412 placed objects referencing a `leafy()` material across nine distinct model files. The mounted high-detail techniques do **not** all omit shadows: `tree.fx` requests alpha test and depth writes; `grass.fx` requests alpha test without depth writes. Measure existing coverage first; avoid duplicate casters, opaque rectangles and far-shadow shimmer. Reuse wind deformation and leave trunks, banners and transparent FX out. [First-party vegetation rendering reference](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-i-geometry/chapter-4-next-generation-speedtree-rendering).
5. **Water that reveals its depth.** The existing [water sheet](../public/js/game/terrain.js) already carries shore depth, warped ripples and a planar reflection. For ordinary water, use angle-dependent reflection and depth-dependent absorption: stand over the bank and see the bottom; look along the same surface and see the reflected tree line. Preserve the map tint, `reflect` setting, subsurface visibility and fog of war. No second mirror, puddles, water simulation or changes to lava/ice. [Water shading reference](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models).
6. **Local ground bounce rather than a map-wide average.** [`soilColor`](../public/js/game/groundday.js) currently weights small mounted texture mips by coverage into one RGB value. Precompute a small world-space grid from the same painted layers, blend it smoothly and let the *underside* of a passing unit pick up local sand, moss or paving color. Use the current estimate as fallback and update affected cells if paving changes. The topside and markings should not change when crossing a ground boundary. This is diffuse reflected color, not full GI.

**Larger follow-up:** bring the [half-float space light pipeline](linear-battle-lighting.md) to land after auditing blend modes. Ground scorch and tread marks multiply the destination, water has separate multiply/add passes, and heat is displacement. They cannot all be converted as emitted RGB. Test a bright flash next to a shaded building under locked exposure, along with water, tracks, foliage edges, minimap/HUD and depth; budget bandwidth/memory on WebGL before enabling it by default.

For each proposed milestone, compare the same pose and time off/on and then a moving battle in mounted Universal on WebGPU and WebGL. Record p95 frame times and representative worst scenes, not just empty-map medians.

## Evidence from this build

- **Stock FoC art:** inspecting the installed `EV_STARDESTROYER.ALO` with [`parseALO`](../public/js/alo.js) showed **3,814 triangles for the named `EV_StarDestroyer` mesh**, not the whole unit or its shadow volume. Its material is `meshbumpcolorize.fx` with authored base and normal textures. The installed base DDS header reports **512 × 512**. Reproduce the mesh count with `node tools/meshinfo.mjs EV_STARDESTROYER.ALO '^EV_StarDestroyer$'` and `SWR_URL` pointing to the mounted stock asset server. No game or mod art is distributed here.
- **Existing assets can already be improved without porting the game.** The [texture showcase](texture-showcase.md) includes a reconstructed 1024px stock hull and an optional Venator refit with authored panel normals and gloss. The stock reconstruction does not change the silhouette or add a complete physically based material. The [art adapter](stock-art-adapter.md) is a reference/preflight tool, not a production donor-model binding; don't assume permission or runtime support for arbitrary Workshop art.
- **Existing orbital light is deliberately broad.** [The probe](../public/js/game/orbitprobe.js) captures six 24px faces once per environment/look. [Its reduction](../public/js/orbitcolor.js) keeps six RGB averages and a bright region. Good for planet bounce in a fleet; it cannot reproduce the shape of a planet's limb sliding across a smooth fitting. [Hull shaders](../public/js/model.js) already use normal/gloss data and a roughness-dependent highlight. [HDR display](hdr-output.md) is not the same thing as keeping all lighting, particles and bloom in scene-linear radiance through to the final exposure. [The current upscalers](battle-upscaling.md) are spatial, not temporal.

## Investment order

| Priority | What to try | Where it pays off / where it doesn't |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A hero hull with authored material response | Model silhouette-changing forms (bridge overhang, engine bells, trench lip); bake smaller fittings into normals and ambient occlusion. Author different roughness for paint vs. exposed metal, a metal mask for actual conductor surfaces, emissive masks for windows and engines. Keep painted armor predominantly nonmetal. Use a repeatable detail texture and decals rather than a unique giant map for each panel. | Biggest expected gain at inspection and command-camera distance. Use screen-space LOD and shared textures; keep original hardpoints, pivots, damage states, unit stats, collision and far meshes. The [Khronos material specification](https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html#materials) is a useful contract for maps and color spaces; the game needn't switch its ALO runtime to glTF. Use self-authored or cleared art. |
| 2. Light those materials coherently | Keep the existing broad probe for diffuse fill. Add one prefiltered **specular environment per battlefield** for near hulls, selected by roughness. Treat direct sun and planet bounce separately so the sun isn't counted twice. Carry scene-linear color through an HDR intermediate, bloom and one SDR/HDR display transform; preserve the HUD and legacy path. | Makes a matte plate and a smooth bracket look different under the same planet. No per-ship reflections capture needed. [Three's PMREM implementation at the shipped revision](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r169/src/extras/PMREMGenerator.js) is the reference; custom WGSL and WebGL shaders must both consume the filtered result. `scene.environment = …` alone will not do this here. |
| 3. Keep detail stable in motion | The [focused bridge/deck shadow](destroyer-flyby.md#close-up-hull-shadows) has now been implemented and measured without raising whole-map shadow resolution. [Stable flyby edges](destroyer-flyby.md#stable-flyby-edges) now reduces close-range crawl with measured current-frame supersampling and no history. A future temporal alternative would need: jitter, per-object/camera velocities, depth rejection, history reset on camera cuts, reactive masks or separate composition for bolts and transparencies. | Fixes crawling panel lines and flashing one-pixel fighters; cannot create missing geometry. A history filter that leaves a trail behind a TIE is worse than the baseline. See [split shadow maps](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-ii-light-and-shadows/chapter-10-parallel-split-shadow-maps-programmable-gpus), [AMD's temporal reconstruction inputs](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-temporal/) and [our fleet pass measurements](fleet-render-passes.md). Not a claim that a native FSR SDK plugs into WebGPU. |
| 4. Apply the method to land, not the space exposure preset | Make a ground reference with one walker and one surface. Prioritize scale-correct near/far terrain normals, roughness, contact at the feet, directional light and depth-dependent atmospheric haze before dense volumetric effects. | Ground wants bounce and atmosphere; black vacuum shadows on a forest floor would be the wrong result. The existing [terrain renderer](../public/js/game/terrain.js) and [land texture showcase](texture-showcase.md) are a starting point. |

## Where NVIDIA and React fit

- **Useful now:** render a slow reference image under matched lighting in Blender Cycles; [OptiX](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/gpu_rendering.html) can accelerate that on supported NVIDIA hardware. Render the *same* asset once offline and once in Universal: if both look flat, revisit the asset; if the reference looks convincing but gameplay does not, investigate the renderer. No player RTX requirement. Asset import/export and camera/light matching are work, not an existing one-click feature.
- **For the highest-quality in-game still:** prototype a progressive, paused **photo mode** in the Fleet Registry or on a frozen battle. The author's [three-gpu-pathtracer](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer) uses a mesh BVH and compute shaders and marks its WebGPU implementation unstable. Pin compatible versions, translate one hull's materials first, and restart accumulation when the camera or scene changes. This is neither native RTX hardware traversal nor a known drop-in for Universal's custom renderer. It is a better first experiment than promising path tracing during active 100-vs-100 combat.
- **Not a visual upgrade by itself:** [React Three Fiber](https://github.com/pmndrs/react-three-fiber) is a React renderer for Three.js. It can organize scene code, not supply new geometry, light transport or material maps. There's no reason to change the battle loop for image quality.
- **Different product decision:** [NVIDIA DLSS Super Resolution via Streamline](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/Streamline/blob/main/docs/ProgrammingGuideDLSS.md) requires native graphics resources, including color, depth and motion vectors. The current web renderer, even inside Tauri, doesn't own the necessary native integration. [Standard WebGPU](https://www.w3.org/TR/webgpu/) is not a portable hardware ray-tracing pipeline; this does not rule out shader-based software ray tracing. [RTX Remix](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/rtx_remix/latest/docs/introduction/intro-overview.html) targets selected legacy games, not this browser reimplementation. Don't promise original retail compatibility either. If high-end native PC becomes a separate goal, evaluate D3D12/Vulkan, hardware RT, denoising and reconstruction **after** the hero asset and scene targets exist. No SDK integration or native performance result was tested here.

## First milestone and stop rules

1. Select one permitted close-detail hull; keep existing battle bindings and a fast switch back to stock. Freeze camera, sun, exposure, planet and team paint. Capture current hull/current light, new hull/current light, then new hull/new light. Use the offline reference to identify what still looks wrong.
2. Compare the bridge silhouette, the skyward and planet-facing plates, and colored engine detail in stills. Then orbit the camera, fly past moving fighters and fire bolts; check for shimmer, ghosting, LOD pops and lost team colors. A still alone cannot pass a temporal change.
3. Verify through the mounted [Universal window](http://127.0.0.1:8590/) at 1080p on WebGPU and the WebGL fallback, close and wide, in a 100-vs-100 battle. Record p50/p95 frame time, draw calls, triangles and texture memory against the same build and hardware. Proposed target: aim for 60 Hz (16.7 ms per whole frame) on a named target PC; if the baseline already misses that, report the delta rather than promising 60 FPS. Keep high-detail LODs only where the improvement is visible. The original research did not benchmark gameplay; [the follow-up milestone](destroyer-flyby.md) reports a live fleet-zoom comparison.
