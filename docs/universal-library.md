# Universal campaign library

One local start screen for stock Forces of Corruption and six separately playable overhauls. It is **not** a merged ruleset, a Steam upload, or a change to your subscribed items. For a saved collection with multiple ordered submods, use **Workshop → Use collection order → Prepare playset → Open playset game**. The separate Workshop launcher is also available. See [tested mods and load orders](installed-compatibility.md) for the compatibility matrix and how to check a different order.

1. Install stock and whichever parent mods you want through Steam. From the checkout root run `npm run universal:build` to import all supported installed parents, or e.g. `npm run universal:build -- 1125571106` for Thrawn’s Revenge alone. Stop Steam updates during import. Stock needs no import.
2. Run `npm run universal` (Windows: **Universal Library.cmd**) and open **http://127.0.0.1:8590/**. The game opens Home with stock Forces of Corruption mounted. There is no launcher shelf or mod selection before the menu.
3. Choose **Play → Single Player → Galactic Conquest**. Choose one of the four era cards. Under **Time-based**, drag the calendar dot to a starting year. The marks are spaced by elapsed years, including fractional years; arrow keys step between supported starts, and Home/End jump to the endpoints. Fall’s prewar FTGU opening has its own labeled stop before the calendar. Choose **Scenario-based** to see scenario maps without a time-period selector. Returning to **Time-based** restores the previous period. An era with only scenarios opens the scenario list directly. Compatible maps appear in one table: **Map | Mod | Planets**. Click **Map**, **Mod**, or **Planets** to cycle ascending → descending → original order. Names sort alphabetically; planet ranges compare the minimum, then maximum, with unknown counts last. Sizes stay under their parent, and sorting does not change the preview or launch target. Expand a map name to see its indented sizes, then choose a size to preview it. The parent shows the smallest to largest playable planet count for the current period. Opening or closing it does not change the campaign. In **Scenario-based**, the full-width **Story**, **Random**, **Sandbox**, and **Miscellaneous** tabs show one list at a time. Empty tabs are omitted. Planet counts under 50 are red, 50–99 amber, and 100+ green. Each end of a range has its own color. Grayed-out map lines and their **Requires [mod]** tooltips identify supported mods without a usable local pack. Click one to view its placeholder submod page, then close it to return. It does not install anything, change your selected map, or supply unverified dates or counts. A range on an individual map means its available variants differ; selecting one shows its exact count. For a scripted selector the count describes its setup map, not a promised final galaxy. **Scenario-based** includes undated, scripted and historical range scenarios without inventing a start year. Click a map to preview it. Switching periods keeps the selected map if it fits; otherwise choose another map before starting. If a map has distinct starts in the same period (such as the 6 ABY Imperial regimes), pick the variant under the map. Below the maps, use **Faction** to choose a supported faction and **Settings** for difficulty, economy, politics and other options. Switching these tabs keeps your choices. **Start campaign** stays in the footer. Only installed or fully materialized campaigns can start. Use the Back chevron beside the title at the top left of the galaxy, or press Escape, to return to mode selection; **Reset** restores defaults without changing faction or map. **Campaign**, **Skirmish**, and **Instant Battle** open directly into their setup boards. New Campaign shows era cards for installed stories. Select an era to see its campaign cards below, with compact **Campaign**, **Variant**, **Mod** and **Planets** columns. The planet count uses the same colors and playable-location data as Galactic Conquest. **Info** opens first. It shows starting credits and the description when available, with ranges where faction starts differ. **Faction** requires an explicit choice even for single-faction stories; switching campaigns clears it. Other factions present in the opening are gray and labeled **Not selectable in this campaign**. **Settings** uses the Galactic Conquest difficulty ticks. The map previews that exact opening. Skirmish and Instant Battle share a galaxy without trade lanes. It draws one marker per named battlefield planet, independent of the number of mods, land/space maps or scenario aliases that use it. Click a world or search by name, mod or domain. Skirmish opens without a selected map. Zoom to reveal small labels that look for free space around nearby worlds. Campaign loaders, test controls and placeholder planet aliases aren't battlefields. Expand a planet row to see compact map titles and source mods under Land and Space headings. Search matches displayed planet names, mod and domain, not names buried in other planets’ TED filenames. Planet portraits stay on the parent rows. Multiple aliases for the same map in the same mod are shown once. The list uses the remaining sidebar height; only nearby rows are drawn, but the scrollbar and search cover every installed field. Home/End and the arrow keys navigate the full result. Search and chart clicks narrow it. Instant Battle uses the same searchable planet list. Choose a map under **Battlefield**, then use **Forces** for ground factions or the fleet editor. In Skirmish the sidebar has **Battlefield**, **Teams** and **Settings** tabs. Each **Player** cell shows its commander over a rising gradient in that seat’s color. Drag a participant to a team, or onto **Create team** to form a new one without adding a bot. Touch and keyboard users can also activate the portrait, then activate a destination. The seat keeps its faction, color and difficulty. Three or more teams require explicitly choosing a generated space field. The footer shows that field’s map image if available, its title, and colored participant icons by team. Click a seat’s **Faction** to replace the galaxy with the commander stage. All installed roster choices share one wraparound ring. Use the arrows beside the top crest to jump by faction. The arrows beside the figures, Left/Right keys and swipe rotate through individual rosters. Search narrows that same ring without assigning anyone. For example, search **Galactic Republic** to compare Bastila Shan, Obi-Wan Kenobi and Yoda across their actual packs. The front figure is larger and brighter. Its name and era dates appear below it, with the source mod on its own line. The top rail shows a static HD faction emblem, not a hologram. The [commander heraldry guide](commander-heraldry.md) lists the unified insignia and their exceptions. These are visual choices, not cross-pack faction aliases: selecting Yoda, Bastila or a Republic-aligned sector representative still assigns their exact original roster. Installed artwork is remastered in private, revision-keyed caches without changing Workshop files. Only the device behind the commander is projected in 3D. Its light and the backdrop gradient share the focused faction’s color, independently of the participant’s team paint. Where there is no verified emblem, Independent Forces use Universal’s open-orbit neutral standard; it is not presented as official mod heraldry. Reduced motion freezes rotation, hover and the light sweep. The outgoing figure stays visible while the next rig loads, then fades away over the land scene and faction gradient. Built-in weapons and hardpoint props use the figure’s own art source and animated sockets; an unarmed character does not get a substitute blaster. There are no portrait placeholders or vehicle exhibits. On entering Teams the current figure starts warming; nearby posed models share one renderer and a bounded cache. Theater art never changes the battlefield or the source of tactical units. Reduced motion holds an authored pose and switches scenery without animation. A named representative is a roster choice, not a hero spawn. Where a named body is not available, the stage and Teams row identify the actual field representative instead of reusing another commander. Linked art is labeled separately from the seat's pack. Portraits come from the installed game or, for the same named person, another installed pack; no live image service is needed. In **Settings**, enter whole numbers for starting credits and unit cap without the old slider ceilings; a cap of 0 uses the faction default. Choose **Assign** to update the selected player’s card without leaving the commander ring. Keep browsing, or select another player. If you’re already previewing a different commander, selecting a player keeps that preview until you assign it. Escape leaves the ring without applying an unassigned preview or changing the battlefield. Your seat and every bot keep independent factions. Allies share team objectives and bases, but deploy and buy units from their own faction. **×** removes an ally or opponent. Choosing a commander from a different mod changes only that seat. Pick a field independently and keep the matchup, including after a profile switch. On a compatible home map, native skirmish factions use their ordinary setup. A visiting or campaign-only faction instead fields a **Detachment**: up to eight distinct tactical unit types with source models, weapons and squad members, recruited at the battlefield’s standard facilities. Availability is separate for Land and Space. Saves require the donor revisions they started with. No donor scripts, campaigns or primary manifests are stacked. For example, choose a stock Abregado-rae map, then in Teams set You to **Sith Empire · Revan’s Revenge** and Opponent 1 to **Trade Federation · Fall of the Republic**. There is no era or profile selection step. A field from another mod opens with the same selection in its owning game. Missing fields are not offered. Ground Instant Battle starts with deployed ground companies and ends on elimination; space retains fleet outfitting and presets. **Start** uses the displayed selection. **Players & options** and **Edit fleets** open the detailed editors; Back returns to Play. Load Game still uses the era picker to find that profile’s saves.

**Start campaign** uses one deployment screen from confirmation through the first galactic frame, even when switching mods. It shows the destination, faction and current loading stage. The decorative Coruscant orbit photograph is read from the local battle-art collection if available; otherwise the screen uses the strategic chart illustration. No photo is bundled, and neither artwork gates launch.

The outer game window stays at the same address. The engine loads only the selected era’s runtime when needed, rather than mixing conflicting Lua or XML in memory. Per-era frames retain the same stable loopback origins as the previous library, including their save stores and workers. Keep ports 8590–8597 available. Change the block with `SWR_UNIVERSAL_PORT`; changing it changes where the browser looks for saves. Built pack manifests need no conversion.

### Repeat the stock ↔ overhaul compatibility check

With FoC installed, the Fall of the Republic parent (`1976399102`) imported and Universal running at **http://127.0.0.1:8590/**, run from this checkout with Chrome and `playwright-core` available to Node:

```sh
node tools/check-stock-overhaul-roundtrip.cjs webgpu
node tools/check-stock-overhaul-roundtrip.cjs webgl
```

Each run selects a Kuat **space Instant Battle** in stock and then in Fall, using the ordinary setup screen. It verifies the map actually launched, that the simulation advances with modeled units, and that a deployed Fall unit type is absent when stock returns. The test compares the mounted Lambda shuttle model and `GameConstants.xml` against SHA-256 digests read independently from each local installation. The filenames are identical but their contents must differ. It also checks Fall’s Venator unit definition and its model are unavailable in stock.

The check writes a real tactical checkpoint to the **same slot ID** in each profile, then returns to stock, reads the original slot and resumes its named battle. Saves are made only in a disposable Chrome context; no installed art, XML or existing player saves are changed. Expect `PASS: stock → Fall of the Republic → restored stock`. A source mismatch after a Workshop update is a reason to check the local import and fixture, not to substitute stock assets. This check covers these two profiles in space Instant Battle, not every campaign script, land battle, multiplayer match or submod load order.

### System settings

Open the title screen’s **System menu → Settings**, or **Options** from the pause menu. **Video** opens first. Under **Graphics Quality → Battlefield appearance**, choose **Enhanced** for relief on textured models, sky fill and grazing light on shaded surfaces, and horizontal glare from bright sources. **Ultra** keeps those effects and adds **Contact shading** (darkens close surfaces where they meet) and **Material highlights** (uses the map light, view angle and authored gloss when available). This screen-space shading cannot see hidden or offscreen geometry. Set Contact shading to zero to skip the depth sampling. Tune each slider separately, or choose **Original** to remove them without touching other settings. For a space-only preset, choose **Graphics Quality → Space battles → Space battle look → Cinematic**. It brings out lit hull panels, keeps the shaded side cooler, and reins in engine bloom. Compare **Cinematic · corrected color** (linear lighting on hull paint) and **Cinematic · orbital lighting** (adds broad reflections of this battlefield) on the same ship without restarting. **Deep space** darkens the map’s distant haze and shaded hull faces while preserving the sun, planet light and bright engine cores. Choose **Use battlefield settings** to switch back immediately, even with custom sliders. Land keeps its own look. These are tactical effects, not new geometry, higher-resolution assets or ray tracing. [HDR output and calibration](hdr-output.md) is separate. **Controls** shows one full-size (104-key ANSI) keyboard, including the navigation cluster and number pad. Select a key to inspect or assign it, or choose **Test keys** to try a shortcut. On narrow screens, scroll the keyboard sideways. **Gameplay → General** includes game speed, battle rules, tactical tweaks, and Battlefront options. Open **Battle setup defaults** there for skirmish credits, caps, bases and fog, Instant Battle fleet budgets and fog, or starting campaign difficulty. **HUD** controls tactical hints, Battlefront instruments and galaxy-map overlays; **Replays and Spectate** controls recording, spectator displays, log filters and the first report graph. **Social** manages the commander network and has a separate matchmaking playlist and network section. **Accessibility** has General (installed game language, reading speed, tooltip timing and reduced menu motion), Subtitles, Colourblindness (faction colours and Battlefront markers), and Text-to-speech (optional narration of focused Settings controls when supported by the browser). Portable player preferences edited here follow you from the title screen into stock and Workshop battles. Untouched profile choices, saves, map and fleet drafts, installed language and texture packs stay local; network identity and presence use the selected relay. **Back** at the bottom right or **Esc** returns to the previous menu. The tabs stay at the top while the list scrolls at the window edge. **Gameplay → General → New Galactic Conquest defaults** lets you preselect eligible rules supplied by installed packs without changing current saves. See the [settings inventory](settings-inventory.md) for the added graphics, sound, Battlefront and accessibility controls and for options that remain campaign-specific. See **Workshop → Mounted content** for the active game and mod order.

For a sharper tactical view, open **Video → Graphics Quality → Image quality**. Set **Texture magnification → Bicubic** to recover crisper transitions in the original colour textures when zoomed in on ships or terrain. It does not replace the art or increase the source resolution. Set **Post-process AA → FXAA** for an inexpensive edge filter, or **Off** if it softens fine detail. **Automatic** uses it when other effects already run and geometry AA is enabled. For high-quality upscaling, try **Battle upscaling → Ultra quality · 90%**, **Upscaling method → AMD FSR 1**, and **Upscale sharpness → 50%**. Drop to 77% for a lighter scene workload. FSR 1 follows edges in the current frame and then sharpens at output resolution; 0% skips the sharpening stage, not the edge reconstruction. **Lanczos 3** and **Bicubic** are alternatives, and **Bilinear** is the softest and simplest filter. If performance is already good, **Native + sharpening · 100%** keeps the original pixels and applies RCAS without upscaling; use the separate AA control for jagged edges. For true supersampling, leave upscaling off and try 1.5×. These are spatial filters, not DLSS, DLAA, frame generation or temporal reconstruction. See the [upscaling notes](battle-upscaling.md) for the render path and its limits. The battle canvas retains the selected output resolution; menus and HUD are not filtered. None of these modes uses AI or modifies installed game files.

For environments, open **Video → Graphics Quality → World environments**. **Orbital atmosphere** uses the planet’s atmosphere color and sun direction to brighten the sunlit rim, and its cloud texture to cast shadows on the dayside. It applies to modeled planets on the programmable shader tiers, not cached portraits or planets painted into a sky texture. **Terrain layering** draws broad color changes and finer surface texture from each painted ground layer, with the fine detail fading at distance. Try 80% alongside Ultra, or use either slider on its own; 0% restores the original treatment without changing the battlefield look.

In **Controls → General**, you can invert mouse look for unit cameras, skip the direct-control camera transition, and change how space control groups and waypoint paths behave. **Gameplay → HUD → Tactical direct control** sets sight size, weapon arc visibility and the mouse reminder. **Video → Details → Player cinematic camera** adjusts cuts and letterboxing, or returns you to where you started. Mission cutscenes are unaffected. Under **Audio → Music and movies**, you can lower music while mission dialogue is playing and adjust when calm music returns after combat.

To configure a solo Battlefront match, choose a **Skirmish → Battlefield → Land** map, open **Settings**, and check **Play this match in Battlefront mode**. Use **Gameplay → General → New solo Battlefront skirmishes** to keep defaults for future matches. Changes on the board apply only to that match; neither path changes campaign or multiplayer rules.

- **Command posts:** Set neutralization and capture time separately. For example, try **2× map time** to dislodge a defender but **0.5× map time** to claim a neutral post. Ordinary build pads keep their map timing.
- **Deployment:** Choose how many friendly squads can serve as spawn points (including Off) and how long their leader must go without a hit. These don't remove your home base or held posts. Direct deployment places you near the squad leader; with **Shuttle deploys** on, the shuttle still uses a landing zone when one is available. You can also set the first wave, repeat interval and passengers per shuttle. The defaults are 3 seconds, 15 seconds and 8 infantry; smaller loads send additional craft in the same wave.
- **Attrition:** Set casualty ticket costs, majority drain, player and bot reinforcement delays, points per hero, and infantry field healing. Turn majority drain off to make casualties the only source of ticket losses.

For fewer bars over a crowded battlefield, open **Gameplay → HUD → Tactical unit health** and choose **Selected or hovered**. You can also widen the bars or hide their shield rows. In **Battlefront radar and compass**, you can keep north at the top, zoom in on nearby contacts, dim the terrain, turn off the view cone or distant post pins, and use compass degrees instead of cardinal letters. The radar and compass settings do not change the deployment map.

For camera speed, wheel direction, controller drift or touch gestures, open **Controls → General**. You can turn off two-finger rotation without losing pinch zoom. In **Gameplay → General → Galactic Conquest autosaves**, you can enable a separate checkpoint every 1, 3 or 5 fiscal cycles of a solo conquest. **Gameplay → Replays and Spectate → Spectator event log** controls how many rows you see, which repetitions are grouped, and whether clicking a deployment stays at the event location or follows its unit. Minimap marker size and galaxy label legibility are in **Gameplay → HUD**. In the same tab you can widen trade routes, hide animated route traffic, or show only your faction’s fleet movement lines. **Battlefront combat feedback** controls sight size and opacity, target health, personal-only elimination reports and score pop-ups. Under **Accessibility**, use **General** to dim land lightning and bombardment lighting, **Colourblindness** to distinguish radar contacts by shape, or **Text-to-speech** to send new battle messages to your screen reader. For larger movie captions, choose **Accessibility → Subtitles** and use the live preview. Campaign opening credits and tech are under **Gameplay → General → New Galactic Conquest defaults**; they only prefill starts with compatible native setup controls.

### How Skirmish commander choices are identified

The directory in `tools/skirmish-commanders.mjs` maps **(installed profile, faction)** to a family and a representative. It never reverse-resolves a roster from a person's name. For example, Fall's `I_BUTTON_NUTE_GUNRAY` represents the Trade Federation, not the entire Separatist Alliance; Remake's `UNITS_HERO_MINORS_REPUBLIC_MZSINJ.XML` identifies Maarisa Zsinj for the Republic Hardliners, not her son for an Imperial roster. Some independent factions have ships and armies but no common ruler. Their command rows remain selectable on exactly the same terms as named commanders.

Portrait keys refer to `MT_CommandBar.mtd` in the installed pack. Supplemental portraits match the named person, not an internal faction key: Clone Wars Ziton Moj can use Fall's `I_BUTTON_ZITON_MOJ`, for instance. Private game/mod pictures are not redistributed. The ring’s land scenes are optional, digest-checked local captures, not launch previews: Gunray can stand before Theed, Wat Tambor on Mustafar, and Tyber Zann on Ryloth whatever TED you selected. If a photograph is missing, the faction gradient remains; the ring never substitutes an orbital picture. Independent Forces, Invaders and Imperial Defectors have distinct interface markers when no distinctive faction crest is verified. A repurposed `Hutts` ID is not evidence that Invaders should use a Hutt cartel logo.

### Click a URL to start Universal (Windows)

From this checkout, register the on-demand link once for your Windows user: `powershell -NoProfile -File tools/universal-game-link.ps1`. Then open `eaw-universal://play` or double-click **Play Universal.url**. Windows starts the local server if needed and opens the title screen in your default browser. Clicking again reuses the same running Universal instance. Your browser may ask permission to open the app. No administrator rights, sign-in task, public hosting or file uploads are involved.

The ordinary `http://127.0.0.1:8590/` bookmark works while the server is running. For a separate instance, pass e.g. `-Port 8600` at registration; the link then always opens `http://127.0.0.1:8600/`. Different port blocks have separate browser saves. The link never stops another listener or silently changes ports. If you move the checkout, remove the registration first and register again at the new location. To unregister: `powershell -NoProfile -File tools/universal-game-link.ps1 -Remove`.

### Start a campaign and choose its path

Each era has one split crest, with the card background shaded from the faction on its left into the one on its right: Jedi blue / Sith red, a red Republic cog on white / Separatist blue, Rebel ember / white Imperial cog on black, and New Republic gold / the same white-on-black Imperial half. Hover a card for the faction names. Gold still marks the selected era. These setup icons need no logo download, Steam cache or extra pack.

The settings panel runs the full height of the right edge. The rest of the window shows the starting galaxy with its faction key. A neutral starfield is visible while the atlas opens. The native sky fades in over it. Selecting a different region eases the camera to that sector; changing years on the same map keeps your chosen zoom and pan. Incoming routes and rings fade in against the shared sky, never over the previous campaign's foreground. The system’s reduced-motion setting skips these transitions. Wheel over a planet to zoom smoothly about the pointer, or double-click its name to center and zoom; drag to pan only when zoomed in. The frame stops at the edge of the galaxy image. With the map focused, +/− zoom and Home restores the whole-galaxy overview; Escape leaves setup.

Pick a faction by its hero card. A gold glow marks the selection. The portraits and emblems come from that storyline’s locally mounted art; a representative is not necessarily a starting unit. Each owner in the map key gets an emblem, a faction unit icon if no emblem is supplied, or a color-coded monogram when neither is available. Difficulty, treasury per planet and objectives are single-choice lists. The economy/politics preset and political control per point sliders snap to the available settings, not arbitrary values. Use the arrow keys on a focused slider to step between them.

Every setup map occupies the same galaxy. Universal uses the galaxy art and perspective from one installed reference chart (Thrawn’s Revenge, Fall, Revan, then stock) and keeps it while changing campaigns. The preview omits the backdrop model’s decorative region borders (such as the Outer Rim and Core). It keeps the starfield, playable hyperspace lanes and ownership rings. When zoom would enlarge the overview beyond its pixel resolution, the native renderer redraws the visible lanes and rings at viewport resolution using the selected map’s own geometry, colors, textures and blending. One anchored detail image covers the close-up; it is discarded on a map change. Gameplay and installed art are untouched. Shared planet identifiers align each profile with the reference chart. On-map and off-map worlds use the same 128-pixel portrait tiles and six-pixel diameter at whole-galaxy fit. Planets scale with the chart camera, not the era’s gameplay `Scale_Factor`. Names have no shadow: they grow more slowly on approach, then taper from 13 to 11 screen pixels at close zoom so they don't crowd the planet. Off-map portraits and their names share a monochrome layer at 20% opacity. They yield to selected planets, ownership rings and names at every zoom. Hovering an off-map dot can reveal its name only where it fits without covering the selected map. Hover follows the pointer as the camera moves. Crowded names keep a usable position and fade in or out when their visibility changes, rather than blinking on each zoom step. Active names render at screen resolution and fade at the viewport edges; names already in view stay steady. Active planets use their selected map’s authored formation under a common scale, rotation and translation; a shared identity does not force a planet back to its reference position. This is a game chart, not a claim of canonical coordinates. Off-chart setup markers are labeled as uncharted. Only the selected campaign supplies active planet art, lanes, rings, owners and the planet count. Gray context worlds never supply extra lanes or playable locations. Nothing changes the simulated map or its saved coordinates. It shows initial ownership, not fog of war, fleets or story changes. Names and ownership remain available while the art loads or if a model cannot be rendered. The solo operation boards reuse this renderer for the selected story opening or the combined battlefield chart. Multiplayer keeps its existing staging.

On the reviewed Fall of the Republic and Revan’s Revenge progressive maps, **Time period** filters the maps and selects a concrete destination. Thrawn’s Revenge also exposes progressive, regional and historical starts where its local selector tables enable that map, faction and date; its two supported 6 ABY Imperial regime destinations appear separately. **Start campaign** goes straight to the displayed scenario on the target pack’s own origin. No placeholder galaxy, scripted reload or transfer of saves. Fixed scenarios keep their authored start. Specialized custom maps, Thrawn’s minor-warlord selector and unrecognized loader revisions are not translated by guessing at Lua or exposing every unlisted XML entry. Use the original selector for those supported by the mod.

**Thrawn custom openings:** Under **New Republic → Time-based → 4 ABY**, pick a Custom layout and its faction. In **Settings → Custom opening**, **Static era** keeps the destination’s own tech; **Progressive era** enables native era progression; **Learner** asks the mod to set up its standard roster, heroes and small start. This is separate from Easy/Normal/Hard. Ordinary FTGU still has only two choices. Other dates, special subfactions and unknown revisions remain in the original selector.

**Thrawn Canon Units:** On the reviewed **New Republic → 4 ABY → Known Galaxy – Small → New Republic** start only, **Settings → Canon Units** can request the mod’s **Enable Canon Units** order. Dismiss the native briefing. Once controls unlock, Universal selects an owned starbase, opens the native Options filter and places one real order. The normal cost, queue, timer, yard requirements and command-staff rules apply. No free ships. **Campaign default** does nothing. If the order can’t be placed, the log tells you; the in-game control remains available.

**Thrawn Fractalsponge Units:** On the reviewed **New Republic era → 4 ABY → Known Galaxy – Small** start, choose **Galactic Empire** or **Greater Maldrood**. In Settings, **Fractalsponge Units** requests one native Options order after the briefing. The Empire unlocks its Impellor and Compellor; Greater Maldrood unlocks its Legator. You still need the normal yard, category, budget and population to build them. Campaign default queues nothing.

**Mercenary contracts (new stock Equal Footing → Rebel Alliance):** Enable **Settings → Mercenary contracts**, then use **Hire corvette · 750 credits** in the galactic droid log. You hire one Rebel Corellian corvette at friendly, uncontested Bespin when its station can offer the ship and you have population room. Service lasts 120 campaign seconds; the next hire opens 60 seconds after that term, even if the ship was lost early. Only the hired ship departs. Moving it or reloading does not renew the term; losses are not refunded. This is a local hire, not a faction roster unlock.

**Emergency fighter support (same stock opening):** Enable the separate **Emergency fighter support** setting. In an ordinary manual defense of your Rebel planet and starbase, **Call X-wing support · 1,500 credits** buys one wave of two Rebel X-wing squadrons. They arrive after 20 tactical seconds if the station and defense hold. Retreating, auto-resolving or losing the station before arrival forfeits the order. The order and countdown survive a tactical save. The squadrons fight only in that battle, and there is no automatic charge or orbital bombardment.

**Starting appearance (native Republic controls):** On **Fall of the Republic → 22 BBY → The Clone Wars → Small → Galactic Republic**, Settings offers clone armor and fleet emblem. On **Revan’s Revenge → 3964 BBY → Known Galaxy → Medium → Galactic Republic**, it offers Revan’s lightsaber color. Explicit choices use the installed mod’s ordinary Options orders after initialization, one cycle at a time with native costs and prerequisites. Leave the Options yard selected while they complete. Campaign default places no order. Supporting models read the current native value in later ordinary campaign battles; hero-specific markings and later manual changes take precedence. Other starts and unreviewed mod revisions do not expose these settings.

**Freight charter (new stock Equal Footing → Rebel Alliance):** In Settings choose the freight objective, then review the offer in the galactic droid log. Accepting puts one loaned Merchant Freighter in orbit at Hoth. Move that ship, with or without escorts, to friendly Bespin. The charter pays 1,500 credits once, subject to the normal treasury cap, at an uncontested destination. A lost freighter earns nothing; if the destination becomes unavailable, return to a secure friendly port without payment. The merchant takes the ship back. The charter is opt-in, survives a save in hyperspace, and does not change normal trade income.

**Derelict recovery (new stock Equal Footing → Rebel Alliance):** Choose **Settings → Objective → Derelict recovery · Sullust**. In the galactic droid log, embark a boarding crew on one of your own Corellian corvettes at Hoth. Move that ship and any escorts to Sullust and fight the space battle manually. If the corvette starts in reserves, use its **Recovery boarding crew** card. **Locate wreck** centers the powerless Nebulon-B; **Plot boarding run** issues a normal move order to the assigned corvette. Keep it inside the gold ring for five uninterrupted tactical seconds and win with it still on the field. Neither another corvette, auto-resolve nor retreat earns the prize. The wreck is scenery, not an enemy or an extra victory condition.

Bring the surviving boarding ship to any secure, friendly starbase away from Sullust. Until refitted the prize is a tow, not a combat ship; losing the corvette loses it. In the droid log review and confirm a full refit (half base build price, **1,100 stock credits**) to add one Nebulon-B at that yard, or release the prize at no cost. Cancelling leaves it in tow. This is one original encounter, not a general boarding ability. No Workshop ship or script is imported.

**Where can I build this?** In a single-player Universal galaxy open **Galactic droid log → Shipyard finder**, or activate a ship on the native space tech tree. Pick a known ship to see its available yards and current blockers, then select a yard to center it. The finder reflects current campaign unlocks, build filters, population, queue room and payment terms. It issues no orders, exposes no hidden worlds and needs no setup switch.

**Rebel flagship (stock Equal Footing only):** On a new **Equal Footing → Rebel Alliance** start, set **Settings → Rebel flagship** to **Designate one Mon Calamari cruiser in the galactic log**. In the droid log choose a cruiser by world, fleet and number, then confirm. It must be an owned cruiser already in orbit; nothing spawns for free. That hull becomes **Flagship Valiant**. The commission cannot be transferred or replaced if the ship is lost. This option is independent of your objective and defaults to off.

Valiant keeps hull and hardpoint damage between space battles. In Reinforcements its gold pennant marks a separate card: hover for its name and condition, then click or drag it onto the map (or focus it and press Enter to begin placement). An ordinary cruiser card will not call Valiant. At an uncontested friendly starbase, open the droid log and choose **Review full repair**. Only **Confirm repair** charges the displayed price; **Cancel** and closing the log are free. The original tariff is half the cruiser’s base build price scaled by the greater of its missing hull fraction and average missing hardpoint fraction, rounded up to a minimum of one credit. Shields and native tactical healing are unchanged.

**Fall custom layouts:** Under **Clone Wars → Scenario-based → Sandbox**, expand **Infinities: Custom Galactic Conquest** to choose FTGU Layout, Small, Medium, Large or Influencers. The reviewed loader offers each layout to the CIS, Galactic Republic and Hutt Cartels. These rows preview the actual destination and start it at the chosen difficulty. Read the opening briefing, then use Fall’s own starting-location, roster and hero choices in the game. **From the Ground Up (FTGU):** In Clone Wars, move the slider to **FTGU · pre-22 BBY** for Fall’s single-planet start. This selects displayed tech 1 with the chosen faction’s adjustment; the gap before 22 is a named opening, not a year estimate. In New Republic, the first tick **4 · FTGU** lists Thrawn’s standard FTGU map first, followed by the other 4 ABY maps. Its **Era progression** setting preserves the mod’s static/progressive choice. Later and specialized FTGU selections remain in the original selector. In Old Republic, **3996 BBY** opens Revan’s Era 1 FTGU for the Sith Empire, Galactic Republic or Mandalorian Clans. The FTGU script reads the local faction’s starting credits to pick its era. The dated start therefore keeps the authored zero-credit default rather than overwriting every faction’s treasury. For the original configurable starting credits, use the same FTGU map under **Scenario-based**.

Universal prepares a private compressed catalog per stock fingerprint, pack revision and language under `campaign-atlas-v1` in the library directory. First use after an update or language change may wait for indexing. After indexing, changing the selection updates the names and ownership immediately. During indexing, each pack’s mounted model geometry, diffuse maps and clouds are rendered into small private PNG sheets under `planet-portraits-v1`. The chooser prioritizes the selected map, then loads the entire installed portrait library (up to four sheets at a time). Once decoded, the same pictures are drawn at display resolution during pan and zoom; they are not an enlargement of a low-resolution map screenshot. Locations whose source model is invisible or particle-only get a neutral shaded marker instead of an invented surface. For ordinary small route textures the private index stores the exact winning bytes per profile; the native renderer can draw the shared sky and switch lanes/rings without reopening each pack’s art mount. Larger texture sets keep the isolated renderer path. Escape releases the worker. For up to 45 seconds the page can retain one opening plate, one opening still and at most four opening portrait sheets for a quick return to the same atlas and viewport. Generated sheets are local game derivatives: do not distribute them. There is no continuously running preview simulation. The shared galaxy render is reused across storylines at the same viewport size; a bounded in-memory cache also keeps the last three campaign views. Zoom and pan crop the rendered chart without loading another map. Browsing does not start another game server, navigate the game or run campaign scripts. A failed launch mount leaves the board and selection intact. No pack rebuild is required just to enable the catalog.

**Authored objectives** and **Campaign defaults** are preselected. The chooser uses listed single-player variants and verified setup-loader destinations. Tutorial and multiplayer use their normal title-menu routes; the separate Workshop launcher is unchanged.

This is a curated catalog, not automatic Lua mixing. A filename or a similar-looking function is not enough to import a faction government, unit roster or scripted story. Each additional system needs a defined effect, compatible targets and a save/restore check.

### What is stripped

A profile is a manifest of the **winning parent layer**: virtual path → SHA-256 object (or a deletion). It does not copy the stock game. MEGs and loose overrides use the original resolver; paths the parent deletes remain absent. Identical bytes, even at different paths or under different names, occupy one object in the library. Different bytes at the same path remain separate, including scripts, localization, campaign XML, textures and models. Every profile keeps its own virtual paths and primary Workshop ID. There is no requirements guess based on filenames, and no attempt to remove individual Lua functions from nonidentical scripts. Functions with the same name need not have the same behavior or callers.

The default build hashes the effective mod files and writes **manifests only**. Playing an installed pack now reads from that pack’s own winning loose files and MEG archive entries. Reads return only bytes verified against the manifest, **without writing them to the object store**. Range requests keep at most 4 MiB of block checksums per server process or worker, not a growing disk cache. Keep the parent installed. If a source changes, a matching older private copy can still serve that revision; without one, the read fails rather than substituting the updated bytes. End the session and rebuild the profile after Steam updates. Other parents need not be installed to play the selected profile.

To make an independent **local** copy of the mod payload, run `npm run universal:build -- --materialize` (optionally append specific primary IDs). This eagerly fills the object store and marks the profile for offline retention, including subsequent rebuilds. A fully materialized pack no longer needs its Workshop folder to play, but **always requires the stock game**. Budget disk space before doing this. Neither build mode deletes any Steam files. Updating packs leaves old objects in place. Browser saves are separate.

The default output is `%LOCALAPPDATA%/EaWBrowserUniversal/library-v1` on Windows (home directory fallback); set `SWR_UNIVERSAL_LIBRARY` to use another private disk. Keep the directory outside the repo and Steam installs. Generated manifests, mod metadata and payloads are local; this repo distributes only the builder, launcher and synthetic tests. Do not redistribute the generated library without the relevant rights and permissions.

### Trim copies from an older installed-mode cache

With Workshop updates paused and older Universal servers closed, run `npm run universal:trim` for a **read-only preview**. `npm run universal:trim -- --apply` repeats the verification under the private library maintenance lock, then removes only reproducible cache objects. Every referring pack must have matching installed bytes for every path; the stored object must match too. Explicit offline packs, complete legacy packs, missing parents, orphaned or unusual entries are kept. Use `npm run universal:audit` to check the remaining store. Do not delete the library, edit manifests, or remove Workshop files. If a build or trim crashed and left `.pack-maintenance.lock`, confirm no maintenance process is running before removing that one lock file. A normal game session with an installed parent no longer refills the object store.

### What was verified

For the six installed primary versions in the first import, the effective mod layers totalled **117.56 GiB**, with **76.02 GiB** of distinct payloads by SHA-256: **41.54 GiB (35.3%)** need not be copied twice, including duplicates within as well as between profiles. This is a comparison with six *separate expanded mod-layer copies*, not observed free disk, a smaller Steam download, or a performance claim. It excludes stock, manifests and filesystem overhead.

**Repeat the mini-mod duplicate audit:** `npm run universal:audit` reads the built manifests and inventories the private `objects` directory. `npm run universal:audit -- --verify` also streams each cached file through SHA-256; it does not fill a thin pack, change files, or read Workshop or save data. `crossProfileBytes` counts a digest once per additional profile; `withinProfileBytes` counts additional references in the same profile. Neither is newly reclaimed disk space. Unexpected entries, bad checksums and objects no current manifest references are reported, not automatically deleted.

On the six packs audited after the station-services release, **33,701 distinct payloads** belonged to multiple profiles. Sharing avoids **37.78 GiB across profiles** and another **3.76 GiB within profiles** relative to separate expanded copies. All **16,709** physically cached objects (**19.53 GiB of file contents**) matched their digest; there were no stray or unreferenced objects to remove. Paths and deletion masks in each mini-mod are references, not extra payload copies. In particular, Universal still queues the native roster and appearance orders. The three EAWX `OptionsHandler.lua` files have different contents, as do the two Awakening freight scripts; the new opt-in stock encounters do not replace those scripts in their original campaigns.

For all six, the full mounted virtual key sets and sizes matched a direct stock + parent mount in English and German; 35 representative whole mod files also matched byte-for-byte. `npm run test:universal-library` checks synthetic cross-profile sharing, divergent scripts, tombstones, MEG and loose inputs, TGA lookup, ranged reads, stale/corrupt bytes, and reading a fully materialized pack with no parents present. The earlier library verified stock and six pack mounts. `tools/test-universal-window.cjs` is an opt-in Chrome check of the native mode buttons, direct conquest board, storyline switching and recovery, inline rules, and exact campaign launch. It requires local built Revan and Awakening of the Rebellion packs and `playwright-core`. Set `SWR_TEST_ALL_ERAS=1` to exercise the board in the other four installed skins as well. These checks do not establish complete campaign or multiplayer parity.
